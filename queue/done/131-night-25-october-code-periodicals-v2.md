## The v2 shape of the two periodicals (the owner's rulings of 24 October 2026, from the brandbook's v2 pages)

These are the fixed templates sessions 2703 and 2704 build. Where the warehouse holds no figure for a slot, the slot says so in the brand's words ("not held", "licensed source needed") or is left out and the report says why; no figure is ever invented.

**The Daily Digest.** The promise: a two-minute read on what changed since the last issue, with the source trail close at hand. Order, fixed:
1. Masthead: the mark, "ERW Daily Digest", the issue date as "26 October 2026", the data cutoff as a time in Eastern Time with its UTC equivalent ("Data through 11:45 ET, 15:45 UTC"). Never "real-time".
2. The opening number: one verified finding in one sentence, with metric, value, unit, geography, period and the comparison basis. Chosen among the day's movers (`brief.movers`) and the day's top story's figure by: consequence for the system or the market first, novelty since the last issue second, source quality third; never the largest percentage move by default; yesterday's metric again only if it moved materially. When no defensible move exists, the opening number is a structural figure from the day's top story (a MW, a USD sum) with its source.
3. The numbers block: four rows, five at most, each with metric and geography, value and unit, change against a stated reference, period, status and source. The default mix: a market price; a physical measure (US48 demand, a flow, storage); a project, queue or capacity figure; a policy, capital or reliability figure. A held or restricted value prints its status, never a zero. The rows keep the keys `scripts/check_digest.py` traces.
4. What moved: two or three stories (not ten), the top of `brief.select` after one topic one item, each with its headline, one why line (the consequence), one limitation if material, and its source. The rest of the top ten and the sector sections fold under a "More" heading (item 5's default: fold, do not cut).
5. The one chart: the chart of the day (`daily_chart`), which deepens the opening finding where a relevant candidate exists; its source line carries publisher, table, period, vintage, ERW retrieval time; if no valid chart can be built, a small table stands in its place and the issue says no chart is shown.
6. The fun fact: one, sourced, separate module at the end; no joke beside a figure; a pun held to its source (item 7's default). The character (below) appears here and nowhere else in the email.
7. Footer: methods, corrections, preferences, unsubscribe.
Length: 350 to 450 words of prose above the fold, excluding source lines, the numbers block, the fold and the footer. Subject line: `ERW Daily | <metric or market>: <value and unit>, <comparison period>`, no figure unless verified with a unit. Send: weekdays 09:00 Eastern (06:00 Pacific); cutoff 08:30 Eastern, named on every issue. Changing the scheduled run's hour is NOT this chain's (no workflow trigger changes); the chain records the recommended time under "For Samuel's ruling" with the workflow line that would change it.

**The Weekly Roundup.** The promise: a one-page executive read on the week's most consequential changes, the evidence behind them, and what merits attention next week. Order, fixed:
1. Masthead: the mark, "ERW Weekly Roundup", the week ending date, the source cutoff in Eastern Time with UTC, a one-line note when the previous ISO week has no issue (item 10's default: yes, one line; W42 has none).
2. The opening number: the number that best explains the week (system scale, persistence, relevance to capital or infrastructure decisions, source strength), said as observed, calculated, estimated or forecast.
3. The week in five numbers (session 806's `roundup.five_numbers`, kept): exactly five, from five different parts of the system where evidence exists (markets; physical flows or demand; infrastructure and capacity; projects or capital; policy or reliability), each with period, unit, status, source vintage; a category with no new comparable observation is replaced by one that mattered, said so.
4. What mattered: three developments ordered by consequence, each with what happened, why it matters to the system, and what the evidence does not establish; sources linked.
5. The chart of the week (session 806's, kept; item 17's related measure stays open for the data machine), with a one-sentence reading note that says what the picture shows, never what to do.
6. Next week: two or three dated items (filings, auctions, hearings, data releases, project milestones) the warehouse or the policy monitor holds with a date; what is scheduled, never a prediction. Where nothing dated is held, the section says "no dated events held this week" and the report lists what a calendar table would need, under "For Samuel's ruling".
7. The fun fact, as the Digest's. The character appears here only.
8. Footer, as the Digest's. The deals and datacenter tables keep item 11's default: confidence 0.7 or more and at least one of operator, site or MW, US rows first, at most 15 a section with a count of the rest and a link.
Length: 500 to 650 words, each development 60 to 90 words, each next-week item one line. Subject line: `ERW Weekly | <plain description of the week>`, with an optional second clause of one verified metric and unit. Send: Fridays 10:00 Eastern with a Thursday 18:00 Eastern cutoff is the brandbook's recommendation; the run is Sunday 23:00 UTC today and stays so (a ruling item for the owner, with the workflow line).
