# MISO: the written layer

## What this grid is and who runs it

The Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) runs the power grid and wholesale market across much of the middle of the country, from Minnesota, Wisconsin and Michigan south through Iowa, Illinois, Indiana and Missouri to Arkansas, Mississippi, Louisiana and East Texas, and into the Canadian province of Manitoba (MISO, https://www.misoenergy.org/meet-miso/about-miso/). It owns no power plants: it schedules the plants that its members own, keeps supply equal to demand, and plans transmission. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) approves its market rules.

Sources: https://www.misoenergy.org/meet-miso/about-miso/ ; https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/delivery-to-consumers.php

## How its market sets prices

MISO runs a day-ahead market, where power for each hour of tomorrow is bought and sold today, and a real-time market, where plants are dispatched every few minutes. Prices are set at each location (locational marginal prices) and summarized at trading hubs; this page uses the Indiana Hub. MISO also holds a yearly capacity auction for the coming planning year.

Sources: https://www.misoenergy.org/meet-miso/about-miso/ ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/wholesale/

## What makes it different

The north-south seam. MISO grew in two parts: the original northern footprint, and MISO South (Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and East Texas), which joined in 2013. The two are tied together by a narrow set of transmission links, so power often cannot flow freely between north and south, and prices on each side can differ. The north has a lot of wind and coal; the south has more natural gas and nuclear.

Sources: https://www.misoenergy.org/meet-miso/about-miso/ ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/dashboard/electric_overview/balancing_authority/MISO

## A short history

- **2001-12:** The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission approves MISO as the first regional transmission organization (RTO) in the United States.
- **2005-04-01:** MISO opens its day-ahead and real-time energy markets.
- **2013-12-19:** Utilities in Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and East Texas join MISO as MISO South.

Sources: https://www.misoenergy.org/meet-miso/about-miso/

## Glossary

- **Balancing authority:** the organization that keeps supply and demand equal in an area. EIA reports MISO as balancing authority MISO.
- **Regional transmission organization (RTO):** a grid operator that runs transmission and markets across several states.
- **Demand:** how much electricity customers are using, in megawatts (MW).
- **Megawatt (MW) and megawatt-hour (MWh):** a megawatt is a rate of power; a megawatt-hour is one megawatt for one hour, an amount of energy.
- **Net generation:** the power plants produce, minus what they use themselves.
- **Day-ahead market:** trades for each hour of tomorrow, agreed today.
- **Real-time market:** the price paid for power at the moment it is delivered.
- **Hub:** an average of many grid locations' prices, used as one reference price; this page uses the Indiana Hub.
- **Seam:** a boundary between parts of the grid where power cannot flow freely.
- **Carbon intensity:** the carbon dioxide released per megawatt-hour of electricity, in kilograms of CO2 per MWh.
