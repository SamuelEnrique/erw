# ISO-NE: the written layer

## What this grid is and who runs it

ISO New England (ISO-NE) runs the power grid and the wholesale electricity market for the six New England states: Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont. It is a nonprofit grid operator that owns no power plants: it schedules the plants that others own, keeps supply equal to demand, and plans the transmission system. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) approves its market rules.

Sources: https://www.iso-ne.com/about ; https://www.iso-ne.com/about/key-stats ; https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/delivery-to-consumers.php

## How its market sets prices

ISO-NE runs a day-ahead market, where power for each hour of tomorrow is bought and sold today, and a real-time market, where plants are dispatched every few minutes. Prices are set at each location and reported for its load zones and one reference price, the Internal Hub, which this page uses. ISO-NE also runs a capacity market, the Forward Capacity Market, that pays resources to be available in a future year.

Sources: https://www.iso-ne.com/about/key-stats ; https://www.iso-ne.com/markets-operations/markets/forward-capacity-market ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/wholesale/

## What makes it different

Winter gas. New England makes much of its electricity from natural gas, but it sits at the end of the pipelines, and in cold snaps homes use that same gas for heat. When the pipelines are full, gas and power prices can jump, and the grid leans on oil, imported liquefied natural gas and power from neighbors. Winter, not summer, is often the season to watch here.

Sources: https://www.iso-ne.com/about/key-stats ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/dashboard/electric_overview/balancing_authority/ISNE

## A short history

- **1997:** ISO New England is created to run the region's grid independently of the utilities (ISO-NE, https://www.iso-ne.com/about/who-we-are/our-history).
- **2003:** ISO-NE starts pricing power by location, with day-ahead and real-time markets (Standard Market Design) (ISO-NE, https://www.iso-ne.com/about/who-we-are/our-history).
- **2008-02:** ISO-NE holds its first Forward Capacity Auction (ISO-NE, https://www.iso-ne.com/about/who-we-are/our-history).

Sources: https://www.iso-ne.com/about ; https://www.iso-ne.com/markets-operations/markets/forward-capacity-market

## Glossary

- **Balancing authority:** the organization that keeps supply and demand equal in an area. EIA reports ISO-NE as balancing authority ISNE.
- **Demand:** how much electricity customers are using, in megawatts (MW).
- **Megawatt (MW) and megawatt-hour (MWh):** a megawatt is a rate of power; a megawatt-hour is one megawatt for one hour, an amount of energy.
- **Net generation:** the power plants produce, minus what they use themselves.
- **Day-ahead market:** trades for each hour of tomorrow, agreed today.
- **Real-time market:** the price paid for power at the moment it is delivered.
- **Internal Hub:** ISO-NE's reference price, an average of locations in the middle of the region.
- **Forward Capacity Market:** ISO-NE's market that pays resources to be available in a future year.
- **Liquefied natural gas (LNG):** natural gas cooled into a liquid so it can travel by ship.
- **Carbon intensity:** the carbon dioxide released per megawatt-hour of electricity, in kilograms of CO2 per MWh.

Sources: https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/about ; https://www.iso-ne.com/about/key-stats
