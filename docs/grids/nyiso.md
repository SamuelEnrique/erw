# NYISO: the written layer

## What this grid is and who runs it

The New York Independent System Operator (NYISO) runs the power grid and the wholesale electricity market for New York State. It owns no power plants and no power lines: it schedules the plants that others own, keeps supply equal to demand, and plans the grid's future needs. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) approves its market rules, and New York's Public Service Commission regulates the utilities that deliver power to homes.

Sources: https://www.nyiso.com/about-us ; https://www.nyiso.com/what-we-do ; https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/delivery-to-consumers.php

## How its market sets prices

NYISO runs a day-ahead market, where power for each hour of tomorrow is bought and sold today, and a real-time market, where plants are dispatched every few minutes at the price of that moment. Prices are set by location and reported for the state's zones, lettered from West (A) to Long Island (K). This page uses the New York City zone (N.Y.C.). NYISO also runs capacity auctions, in which New York City and Long Island must buy capacity located inside them.

Sources: https://www.nyiso.com/what-we-do ; https://www.nyiso.com/energy-market-operational-data ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/wholesale/

## What makes it different

The downstate constraint. Much of New York's cheaper and cleaner power, from hydro, nuclear and wind, is upstate, while most of the demand is in New York City and on Long Island. The transmission lines between them can carry only so much, so downstate prices are usually higher and downstate must keep enough power plants close by, most of them burning natural gas. New lines to bring more power into the city are one of the state's main energy projects.

Sources: https://www.nyiso.com/what-we-do ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/dashboard/electric_overview/balancing_authority/NYIS

## A short history

- **1999-12-01:** NYISO takes over running New York's grid from the New York Power Pool.
- **2003-08-14:** The Northeast blackout leaves New York City and much of the state without power.
- **2021-04-30:** The last reactor at the Indian Point nuclear plant, north of New York City, shuts down.

Sources: https://www.nyiso.com/about-us ; https://www.nyiso.com/what-we-do

## Glossary

- **Balancing authority:** the organization that keeps supply and demand equal in an area. EIA reports NYISO as balancing authority NYIS.
- **Demand:** how much electricity customers are using, in megawatts (MW).
- **Megawatt (MW) and megawatt-hour (MWh):** a megawatt is a rate of power; a megawatt-hour is one megawatt for one hour, an amount of energy.
- **Net generation:** the power plants produce, minus what they use themselves.
- **Day-ahead market:** trades for each hour of tomorrow, agreed today.
- **Real-time market:** the price paid for power at the moment it is delivered.
- **Zone:** one of the areas NYISO reports prices for; N.Y.C. is New York City.
- **Congestion:** when transmission lines are full, so cheaper power cannot reach where it is needed and prices differ by place.
- **Peak and off-peak:** peak hours are weekday daytime hours when demand is high; the rest are off-peak.
- **Carbon intensity:** the carbon dioxide released per megawatt-hour of electricity, in kilograms of CO2 per MWh.
