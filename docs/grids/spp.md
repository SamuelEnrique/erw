# SPP: the written layer

## What this grid is and who runs it

The Southwest Power Pool (SPP) runs the power grid and wholesale market across the central Great Plains, from the Dakotas and Montana south through Nebraska, Kansas and Oklahoma to parts of Texas, New Mexico, Arkansas and Louisiana (SPP, https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/). It owns no power plants: it schedules the plants that its members own, keeps supply equal to demand, and plans transmission. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) approves its market rules.

Sources: https://www.spp.org/about-us/ ; https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/ ; https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/delivery-to-consumers.php

## How its market sets prices

SPP's market, the Integrated Marketplace, has a day-ahead market, where power for each hour of tomorrow is bought and sold today, and a real-time market, where plants are dispatched every few minutes. Prices are set at each location (locational marginal prices) and summarized at trading hubs; this page uses the SPP North Hub. SPP has no capacity auction: its members must show they have enough capacity themselves.

Sources: https://www.spp.org/about-us/ ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/wholesale/

## What makes it different

Wind. The Great Plains are among the windiest places in the country, and on windy nights wind can supply a large share of SPP's power (SPP, https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/), pushing some prices to zero or below. Because the wind is strongest far from cities, getting it to customers depends on long transmission lines, and SPP curtails wind when the lines are full: the curtailment tracker on this site shows it.

Sources: https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/ ; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/dashboard/electric_overview/balancing_authority/SWPP

## A short history

- **1941:** Utilities in the region pool their power to keep an aluminum plant in Arkansas running for the war effort; SPP grows from this pool (SPP, https://www.spp.org/news-list/spp-celebrates-20-years-as-an-rto-with-growth-and-added-value-for-members/).
- **2014:** SPP launches the Integrated Marketplace, with day-ahead and real-time markets (SPP, https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/).
- **2021-02-15:** During Winter Storm Uri, SPP directs utilities to cut power to some customers to protect the grid (SPP, https://spp.org/documents/65037/comprehensive%20review%20of%20spp's%20response%20to%20the%20feb.%202021%20winter%20storm%202021%2007%2019.pdf).

Sources: https://www.spp.org/about-us/ ; https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/

## Glossary

- **Balancing authority:** the organization that keeps supply and demand equal in an area. EIA reports SPP as balancing authority SWPP.
- **Demand:** how much electricity customers are using, in megawatts (MW).
- **Megawatt (MW) and megawatt-hour (MWh):** a megawatt is a rate of power; a megawatt-hour is one megawatt for one hour, an amount of energy.
- **Net generation:** the power plants produce, minus what they use themselves.
- **Day-ahead market:** trades for each hour of tomorrow, agreed today.
- **Real-time market:** the price paid for power at the moment it is delivered.
- **Integrated Marketplace:** SPP's day-ahead and real-time markets.
- **Hub:** an average of many grid locations' prices, used as one reference price; this page uses the SPP North Hub.
- **Curtailment:** turning down wind or solar that could have produced, because the grid cannot use or move the power.
- **Carbon intensity:** the carbon dioxide released per megawatt-hour of electricity, in kilograms of CO2 per MWh.

Sources: https://www.eia.gov/electricity/gridmonitor/about ; https://www.spp.org/about-us/fast-facts/
