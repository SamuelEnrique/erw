### Themes: the areas the call names

The call lists eight areas (quoted above). The Accelerator's own page on them, `https://sustainability-accelerator.stanford.edu/about/flagship-destinations`, counts six "flagship destinations" and two cohorts that serve all six:

- "Six "flagship destinations," or sustainability targets, guide our work and illustrate where we see the greatest potential for impact at a global scale."
- "We aim to benefit one billion people by 2035 and reduce and remove gigatons per year of greenhouse gas emissions by advancing climate adaptation, reliable and affordable electricity and grid systems, sustainable food production, responsible greenhouse gas removal, sustainable industry, and widespread access to clean water."
- Climate adaptation: "Improve the lives of one billion people by 2035 by enhancing adaptive capacity, strengthening resilience, protecting ecosystems, and reducing vulnerability to climate change, with a focus on climate-related extreme events."
- Electricity and grid systems: "Reduce gigatons per year of greenhouse gas emissions from the electricity sector globally by 2035 while driving toward zero-GHG, reliable, flexible, and affordable electricity for all by 2050."
- Food and agriculture: "Reduce gigatons per year of greenhouse gas emissions from food and agriculture systems by 2035 while supporting the global goals of ending hunger, securely supplying healthy diets, and ensuring sustainable food production."
- Greenhouse gas removal: "Remove gigatons of greenhouse gases from the atmosphere per year by mid-century through an integrated approach that will support the development of needed technological, governance, financial, and other societal innovations."
- Industry: "Reduce gigatons per year of greenhouse gas emissions from industry by 2035 by launching solutions that are market-competitive, sustainable, and scalable."
- Water: "Ensure availability, resiliency, sustainable management, and access to clean water for all people, agriculture, industry, and ecosystems while minimizing greenhouse gas emissions by 2050."
- "Two cohorts are dedicated to accelerating progress toward all six flagship destinations."
- Biological solutions: "Leverage biological systems to create and enhance sustainability solutions across all flagship destinations."
- Planetary intelligence: "Address gaps in observation and measurement through sensing technologies and artificial intelligence to provide data-driven actionable insights across all flagship destinations."

### 4.2 Letter of intent: questions and limits (pages 35 to 37, "Section 3: Questions")

Under "Team Organization":

So the 2025 letter of intent had no single page limit for the whole letter. Its stated limits were 150 words (question 3), one uploaded page including figures (question 4), and 300 words each (questions 5 to 8): 1,350 words plus one page, plus the team tables and three suggested reviewers.

### 4.3 How a letter of intent was screened (page 5, "Letter of Intent (LOI) stage")

- "All teams are required to submit an LOI. LOIs are non-binding, and should be submitted with the understanding that proposals may evolve prior to the submission of a full proposal."
- "The Accelerator will review all submitted LoIs. Projects that meet the eligibility criteria and align with the mission and flagships of the Accelerator (see Sections I and VI) will be invited to submit a full proposal. We prioritize projects with the potential for large-scale impact (e.g., reducing greenhouse gas emissions by gigatons per year or improving the lives of one billion people) and speed (externalizing the solution from Stanford within 1-3 years)."
- "Multiple LoIs from the same PI or team are allowed, provided they are for distinct projects, though it is unlikely that a PI will be awarded more than one award."
- "All materials must be submitted through the application portal."
- Proposal stage: "Teams that are invited to submit a full proposal will have approximately five weeks from Accelerator approval of the LOI to submit a full proposal."

### 4.4 Evaluation criteria (pages 4 and 5, "VII. Evaluation criteria")

"All proposed projects will be evaluated against the following criteria:"

- "Transformative Potential / Innovation. Accelerator projects should be novel and ambitious in scope. Reviewers will be familiar with target markets and will evaluate proposals in relation to existing solutions."
- "Impact. Impact goals are specific to the flagship and identified in each RFP. Assessment will be based on (1) clarity of description of potential impact, (2) intention and ability to quantify impact, (3) identification of beneficiaries."
- "Scalability. While the Accelerator recognizes the importance of small scale solutions, we seek to fund solutions that can scale rapidly, in keeping with the scale and urgency of global sustainability challenges." The next sentence, quoted in fragments around its dashes: "Evaluation will focus on the solution's potential to scale", "whether through growth or replication", "and its ability to deliver meaningful benefits for both people and the planet."
- "Feasibility. For a solution to scale successfully, it must first show evidence of traction. Feasibility assessment will consider (1) team constitution, (2) technical, economic, and political feasibility, (3) projected market demand and (4) any other key dynamics (e.g. recent market collapses, political instability in target regions)."

Who reviews (page 5): "Submitted proposals will be circulated to a team of external reviewers, drawn from relevant disciplines, including investment, industry, academia, policy, and other areas directly relevant to the proposal's subject matter." And (page 1): "Proposals are reviewed by external experts and the Accelerator's senior team."

### 4.5 Eligibility (page 4, "VI. Eligibility for grant awards")

- "To be eligible for a Sustainability Accelerator grant award, teams must be led by a Stanford faculty or Stanford and SLAC researchers who qualify as a Principal Investigator (PI) according to Stanford University Policy."
- "It is strongly recommended that the team include or plan to include a designated postdoc or student lead for the project (the postdoc/student lead may be on the team already or a planned addition). This student/postdoc will become the point of contact with the Accelerator for entrepreneurial development."
- "The Accelerator team has found that teams are most successful when the faculty PI is strongly engaged and when the faculty lead/co-leads designate and support a post-doctoral scholar or student to hold primary responsibility for driving the Accelerator project."
- "Teams must first submit a Letter of Intent (LOI); the Accelerator will confirm that the team is eligible to submit a full proposal based on the LOI."
- "Multiple LOIs from the same PI or team are allowed, provided they are for distinct projects. However, Accelerator projects are intended to progress with speed and will require a significant time commitment from the PI and team members, so the Accelerator is unlikely to fund more than one project per team, per year. Similarly, faculty are encouraged to serve as PI for no more than two projects, though they can serve as co-PI without limit."

A difference between the two years that is visible on public pages: the 2025 RFP says a postdoc or student lead is "strongly recommended"; the 2026 funding page says "Teams must include" one.

- "Successful projects are awarded funding and supported for a one-year period with bespoke resources, including connections, access to training programs, market insights, financial modeling, piloting, and coaching."
- "At the end of Year 1, projects are re-assessed and may receive additional support. The same process is undertaken at the end of Year 2. Our intent is to help projects externalize from Stanford in three years or less, positioned for success."

## 5. What the call says about data, open science, AI and energy

### 5.1 On the 2026 public pages

Electricity and grid systems cohort page, `https://sustainability-accelerator.stanford.edu/cohorts/electricity-and-grid-systems`:

- "The global electricity sector produces more GHG emissions than any other sector and it is slated to grow rapidly to meet rising demand and to replace fossil fuel-powered vehicles, appliances, and industrial processes with electric options."
- "Solutions are needed across global electricity systems, including generation, transmission and distribution; operation and optimization; storage; policy, regulation and governance; markets, and business models."
- "Projects in the electricity and grid cohort encompass a diverse range of technology and policy approaches, showcasing advancements in photovoltaic manufacturing, grid optimization, battery development, microgrid expansion, and utility strategies for wildfire mitigation."

Planetary intelligence cohort page, `https://sustainability-accelerator.stanford.edu/cohorts/planetary-intelligence`:

- "This cohort is working toward all of the Accelerator's targets, or "flagship destinations," by addressing gaps in the observation system, enhancing measurement capabilities, and delivering planetary-scale, data-driven insights through advanced techniques like innovative sensing technologies and AI."
- "To drive meaningful action, we must collect high-quality data, analyze it effectively and efficiently, and transform insights into practical solutions."
- "By providing timely, science-based information, we enable policymakers, business leaders, civil society, and the public to address sustainability challenges at speed and scale."
- "This cohort addresses critical areas, including GHG-R, climate adaptation, electricity and grid systems, industry, water, food and agriculture, biodiversity, and planetary health."

A precedent in the Accelerator's own portfolio, `https://sustainability-accelerator.stanford.edu/project/data-commons-climate-energy-and-sustainability` (listed under the electricity and grid systems cohort with "Status: Graduated"):

- Title: "Data commons for climate, energy, and sustainability". Team as printed: Ram Rajagopal, Arun Majumdar, Gretchen Daily, Noah Diffenbaugh, Alexandra Konings, David Lobell.
- "This project developed the Stanford Data Commons, an open-source platform that combines university data with Google's Data Commons to unlock sustainability research for public use. The platform allows users to query, visualize, and download datasets in real time on topics such as solar installations, air quality, and climate change projections. One such application is analyzing electrical grid data to identify unused capacity, enabling AI companies to optimize existing infrastructure rather than build new fossil-fuel plants to power their data centers."
- "Building on insights provided by the Stanford Data Commons, GridCARE launched in 2024 as an essential one-stop power solution for AI data centers. The company's AI-powered proprietary platform identifies and unlocks previously untapped grid capacity to accelerate AI infrastructure deployment."

Other project titles on the two cohort pages that concern data platforms or grid data (titles only, as printed): "Ocean data commons: Making coastal and ocean data insights accessible"; "The natural capital data and analytics platform: Transforming sustainability decisions at scale"; "An integrated platform for grid reliability, affordability, and sustainability"; "ReliAdapt: AI-driven power reliability assessment and end-user adaptation platform for sustainable energy transitions"; "ML-driven optimization for the 'Unit Commitment' problem in power systems"; "Feasibility study of an evaporative cooling (with water recovery) for energy-efficient and sustainable operation of edge data centers"; "Rewarding reliability: Market reform policies for a clean, resilient grid".

## 6. Facts that bear on who can submit, stated without comment

- The principal investigator must be PI-eligible under Stanford policy (2026 funding page and 2025 RFP, both quoted). Stanford policy limits that to Academic Council and University Medical Line faculty, with exceptions that need approval (quoted in section 2).
- A student or postdoc is the team's designated lead and point of contact (2026: "must include"; 2025: "strongly recommended").
- In 2025, undergraduates, graduate students, postdocs and "non-PI-eligible staff" were named as welcome team members (section 4.2).
- In 2025, external collaborators were "not required" for selection and expected "early in the project"; three external reviewers were to be suggested by the team (section 4.2).
- In 2025, a full proposal was by invitation after the letter, about five weeks later, and needed a departmental PDRF approved by the proposal deadline (sections 4.3 and 4.7).
- In 2025, the stated screen at the letter stage was eligibility, fit with the mission and flagships, "large-scale impact" and "speed (externalizing the solution from Stanford within 1-3 years)" (section 4.3).
