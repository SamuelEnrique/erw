# Findings brief: storage, duration, the evening shoulder, battery revenue, California's imports and the December 2025 data break

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), 4 October 2026 (session 89). The ten strongest findings the warehouse holds on these six subjects. Each is one sentence, its number, the table and check key behind it, and one caveat. California means the California ISO's grid (CAISO) and Texas means ERCOT's throughout.

**How to check a number.** A check key names one row: `series|<table>|<entity>|<variable>|<time>`. Read the table (`erw.read("<table>")`, or the CSV) and find the row with that entity, variable and `ts_utc`; its `value` is the number after the equals sign. A key that begins `bs|` is a figure of the page "What a battery earns": the page's own statistic, computed from the rows of `battery_stack_monthly` by `site/lib/batterystack.ts` for the battery named in the key, and recomputed by `site/scripts/check-values.mjs`. `tests/test_session89.py` reads every key in this file and compares it with the tables. No finding here rests on anything but those rows.

## Storage

### 1. The US battery fleet grew by 48 percent in twelve months.

- **Number:** 54,489 MW operating in August 2026, 17,689 MW more than the 36,800 MW of August 2025.
- **Table:** `storage_buildout_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `series|storage_buildout_monthly|us:total|battery_operating_mw|2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` = 54488.9
  - `series|storage_buildout_monthly|us:total|battery_operating_mw_net_added_12m|2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` = 17688.7

## The evening shoulder

The shoulder is the run of evening hours in which net load (demand less wind and solar) stands above its mean for the day. "Hours needed" is the shoulder's energy above that mean, over the battery fleet's power: how long the fleet would have to run at full power to carry all of it.

### 4. On the average evening Texas's shoulder asks about twice the hours its batteries hold.

- **Number:** 3.17 hours needed on the average evening of 2026; the fleet stores 1.65 hours and covers 1.58.
- **Table:** `shoulder_hours_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_mean_shoulder_hours_needed|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 3.1742
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_end_fleet_hours|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 1.649
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_mean_shoulder_hours_covered|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 1.5823
- **Caveat:** The measure sets the fleet against all of the evening's energy above the daily mean; a narrower measure of the same evenings, the energy above the midpoint between the mean and the peak, asks 1.01 hours (`series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_mean_shoulder2_hours_needed|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 1.0077).

### 5. On Texas's ten hardest evenings the shoulder asks four times what the batteries hold.

- **Number:** 6.58 hours needed on average over the ten worst days of 2026, 8.61 at the worst; the batteries discharged the equivalent of 1.17 hours on those days.
- **Table:** `shoulder_hours_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_worst10_mean_shoulder_hours_needed|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 6.5791
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_worst10_max_shoulder_hours_needed|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 8.6069
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_worst10_mean_battery_hours|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 1.1747
- **Caveat:** The ten are the worst of January to September 2026 only; the year is not over, and 2025's ten averaged 8.88 hours (`series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:ercot|year_worst10_mean_shoulder_hours_needed|2025-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 8.875).

### 6. In California the batteries now match the average evening.

- **Number:** 3.59 hours needed on the average evening of 2026; the fleet stores 3.46 hours and covers 3.44.
- **Table:** `shoulder_hours_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:caiso_own|year_mean_shoulder_hours_needed|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 3.5854
  - `series|shoulder_hours_monthly|iso:caiso_own|year_end_fleet_hours|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 3.4584

- **Caveat:** This is CAISO's own supply data (the entity `iso:caiso_own`), nine months of 2026, and its load is the load CAISO's supply serves, not EIA's demand; it is not comparable hour for hour with the EIA-based rows of earlier years.

## Battery revenue

### 7. A four-hour battery in Texas did not cover its debt on the last twelve months' prices.

- **Number:** USD 81.40 per kW from October 2025 to September 2026, 33 percent of it from ancillary services; debt coverage 0.88 times.
- **Table:** `battery_stack_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|l12_kw:total` = 81.4
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|l12_share:ancillary` = 33
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|cover` = 0.88
- **Caveat:** This is the upper bound (perfect foresight, reserves paid and never called), and the debt payment is a cost assumption, Lazard's midpoint with 60 percent borrowed at 8 percent over 20 years, not any project's.

### 8. One month is more than half of everything that battery would have earned in Texas since 2018.

- **Number:** February 2021 is 57 percent of all the revenue in the months held; an average year is USD 614.58 per kW with it and USD 269.68 without.
- **Table:** `battery_stack_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|top_share` = 57
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|avg_kw:total` = 614.58
  - `bs|grid=ercot&dur=4&strat=foresight&mw=100&fom=22&ds=6783357|avg_without_kw:total` = 269.68
- **Caveat:** The model assumes the battery was available through Winter Storm Uri and that its reserves were never called; in the storm they were called, and a real battery would have sold its energy and run down.

## The December 2025 data break

### 10. Since 16 December 2025 EIA's own figures for California no longer add up, and the gap is twelve points of demand.

- **Number:** Imports measured as demand less generation were 19.1 percent of demand in October 2025 against 18.4 by interchange, a gap under one point; in January 2026 they were 40.6 against 28.5.
- **Table:** `ba_supply_monthly`
- **Check keys:**
  - `series|ba_supply_monthly|eia930:CISO|net_import_balance_share_pct|2025-10-01T00:00:00Z` = 19.0825
  - `series|ba_supply_monthly|eia930:CISO|net_import_total_interchange_share_pct|2025-10-01T00:00:00Z` = 18.3617
  - `series|ba_supply_monthly|eia930:CISO|net_import_balance_share_pct|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 40.623
  - `series|ba_supply_monthly|eia930:CISO|net_import_total_interchange_share_pct|2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` = 28.4724
- **Caveat:** The gap is a change in what EIA-930 reports as California's generation, not power that went missing: the cause is described in `docs/methods/eia930_caiso_break.md`, and from that date the ERW takes California's generation from CAISO's own data and its imports from the interchange figure.
