# State of the platform, 4 October 2026

Every tool of the Energy Research Warehouse's site as it stands at the end of 4 October 2026 (session 112): its address, whether it is open to visitors or in review, what it answers, its data and how fresh it is, its weakest point, and what would make it ready to share. Then the ten things most worth doing next, ranked.

The site: `https://erw-flame.vercel.app`. A page in review answers the short "in review" page to a visitor and opens in the internal view (`/internal/unlock?token=<the internal token>`). Status is `site/lib/release.ts`, read today: the site's list of pages (`site/lib/pages.ts`) and the four live methods notes make **71 addresses, 11 of them live** (six tools, the terms and the four notes).

**How to read "fresh".** The daily run on GitHub (14:00 UTC) refreshed the daily tables today; "to" is the last day or month a table holds (`warehouse/metadata/coverage.csv`, 132 tables, 112 public and 20 internal, 16,048,958 rows). A page that reads a copy built by hand says so: it is as fresh as its last build, and nothing rebuilds it.

**What was checked today.** "Checked today" means a session of this chain opened the page on production in the internal view and it rendered with data; "in a browser" means its controls were driven in a real browser. Pages not marked were not opened today and are described from the repository's records, not from a new look.

## The ten things most worth doing next

Ranked by what each buys for the people the platform is for, against what it costs.

1. **Mend the battery page's daily refresh.** Today's daily run could not pull ERCOT's reserve prices: the yearly file's zip held an Excel file (`DAMASMCPC_2026.xlsx`, posted 4 October) and the connector expects one CSV (`warehouse/connectors/ercot_as_prices.py`, `parse_year`). The battery step was skipped, so a live page reads the build of 3 October and, if ERCOT goes on posting the year that way, will do so every day until the connector reads what ERCOT now posts. Whether today was a single day's file or the new form is not known: finding out is the first step. Nothing on the page moved and nothing was filled. It is first because it is the one thing here that gets worse by itself. It was found while writing this document and not mended: it needs a request to ERCOT, and this chain was allowed three named pulls.
2. **Measure what real batteries were awarded.** The live battery page is open to visitors and its honest sentence is "what real batteries earned is not in this warehouse". Session 108 found the data: ERCOT's day-ahead awards by resource, public, about 2 GB for every day the file exists. One more zip finds its first day; then a table. It turns the platform's best-known caveat into its best-known result. The real-time side (23 GB, node prices) is a second decision.
3. **Rule on MISO's terms.** One ruling unblocks six places at once: the price board, where power is cheap, the seller tab's second version, the network's price ring, and the older pages that still show MISO while new ones hide it. Today the site says two different things about the same publisher.
4. **Apply the impossible-hours screen to the three tables behind live pages** (`cost_of_power`, `ba_supply`, `ai_power_regions`): one line each, the hours are listed, the before and after is a trial build away. A known faulty number behind a live page is worse than a missing one. It moves live numbers, so it needs your word.
5. **Put the hand-built copies on a schedule.** Seven review pages read a file someone built by hand today (the network's replay, the mix, the price comparison, demand growth, the curtailment profile, the project map, the large-load status). None will say it is stale. A monthly job, and one line in the daily run for the replay.
6. **Open the pages that are ready**, each with its one sentence of weakness on it: the queue, demand growth, where power is cheap, the customer's battery, known data faults, the price board's third version. Six tools for no new work; the fault page first, because it makes the rest credible.
7. **Fix California's carbon intensity** for the ten months without hydro and the hours stamped late. Both are in the register as held as published, behind `/emissions` and the cost of power's carbon. Measure first, then leave out or mark.
8. **Give Ask ERCOT its price history**: the daily summary of hub prices (about 51,000 rows) instead of 3 million, and ask the reserve price question again. Until then its commonest question, a past price, is answered "not here".
9. **Release a Redivis version.** The ERW calls itself citable, and no released version exists to cite. It is one click after reading the diff.
10. **Make the network's third version the live page**, after item 3 and with the replay in the daily run. It is tested further than any other page here, and it answers a question the live page cannot: any day since 2019.

Next after these. Run the site's tests where its packages are: three Node-backed tests skip on GitHub because the workflow runs tests before installing the site; one data test fails only on the data machine; and two of this chain's deploys failed on tests that had never run there. Moving one step in the workflow closes that.

Then: ERCOT's large-load requests as numbers (ask ERCOT); PJM's price license; the other balancing authorities by name on the map and in demand growth; planned batteries' MWh from the annual EIA-860.

- **A fault of timing in the game passed ten plays on this machine and failed on production.** A check that passes here is not a check of production; the plays are now run there, with the rare case forced.
- **A live page changed three times without being meant to** (the count on `/terms`, the home page's "Last refresh", a section on a methods page); each was found by the snapshot comparison and two were put back within the hour. The third, "Last refresh", is still as session 103's load left it and corrects itself with tomorrow's daily run.
