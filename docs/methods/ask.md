# Ask the warehouse: method

`warehouse/chat/` (the loop and its tools in Python, `ask.py`, `tools.py`), `site/lib/chat/` (the same in TypeScript for the site's `/api/ask`), the Ask panel `site/components/ask/AskPanel.tsx`. The account of how it was built, session by session, is `warehouse/chat/README.md`; Ask ERCOT's own rules are [`ask_ercot.md`](ask_ercot.md) and the spending ceilings [`ask_limits.md`](ask_limits.md). This note states the rules an answer is held to as they stand after the chain of 23 October 2026 (sessions 1108 to 1125), each dated.

## The rule

The AI layer never states a number it did not fetch. Every figure in an answer must be a figure a tool returned in that turn, at the precision the answer writes it or a coarser one (a result of 25.1834 may be written 25.18 or 25.2); every cited table must be one a tool read. A draft that fails is sent back once with what failed named; a second failure is refused.

## The tools

| Tool | What it returns |
|---|---|
| `list_tables` | every table with its sector, license, grid, interval, first and last time, rows and source report |
| `describe_table` | a table's shape, columns, entities, variables, units, span, source reports and license |
| `query` | one aggregation (`latest`, `mean`, `median`, `min`, `max`, `percentile`, `count`, `sum`) of a table's rows, filtered by entity, variable, window and the table's own columns, grouped by time, entity or a text column; since session 3297 also by a value threshold (`threshold`: `above`, `below` or both, strictly), so `count` is the number of intervals past a figure |
| `compare` | two queries side by side, with the difference and the ratio |
| `board_rank` (23 October 2026, session 1122) | every hub's newest value of one of the price board's measures (`price_board_latest`, `price_board_spreads`), ranked highest, lowest or by the size of the move, each value with its own date; it is `query` (latest by entity) and a sort, so it agrees with the board's tables |

A grid's scoped chat reads the tables the rule of session 314 finds for the grid (`docs/grids/grid_tables.json`) and the grid's rows only.

## The checks on every draft (23 October 2026)

- **Numbers** (session 20 on): every number of the answer is in a tool result, the question or a tool argument, at the answer's precision. Digits after a letter and a hyphen are a name, not a figure: EIA-930, EIA-860M, COVID-19 (session 1110; session 320's run sent four right drafts back for the 930 of EIA-930).
- **Money units** (session 1110): a figure written with a money unit (USD per MWh, per MW, per kW, per kWh, with USD, US dollars, dollars or a dollar sign) must match a returned figure in that unit. A tool number's unit is its own key's (`..._usd_per_mw`, `value_usd_per_kw`), else the nearest `unit` or single `units` above it. A draft is sent back only when every returned number it matches is in another unit (81,402.19 written as USD per kW when the tool gave USD/MW). A number no result places in a unit, a result over mixed units, a unit with no money and a compound unit (MWh per MW, USD per MW-month) are not judged by this check. **Percent and MW too** (23 October 2026, session 1144), measured first on the 119 stored answers with their tool records: 19 figures in percent and 17 in MW were judged and no right answer was sent back; such a figure is judged only against tool numbers in a unit the check knows. MWh is not judged: four right answers wrote "MWh" before "per MW", a unit written apart.
- **Citations**: every cited table was read; an empty or uncited answer is sent back ("not in the warehouse" needs no citation).

## The refusal (23 October 2026)

After the second failure the answer is: "The warehouse does not hold that figure: the numbers in my draft could not all be traced to a warehouse query. Try asking for one value, one table and one period at a time." (session 1110), and one sentence saying why with no figure in it, for example "Why: the draft was checked twice and a figure in it was in no tool result." (session 1111). The untraced figure is never shown to the reader. The panel heads such an answer "The warehouse does not hold that figure" (session 1115).

## The limits (23 October 2026)

- **At most 12 tool calls** a question (8 until session 1111: 11 of session 320's thirty used all 8). The route's time limit is 120 seconds.
- **The tool calls and their results are recorded** in every answer of an evaluation (a request admitted by its header and the server's `ASK_EVAL_TOKEN`, session 314), and in none of a visitor's (session 1111). `warehouse/chat/eval/eval.py` asks with the record on.

## A held grid (23 October 2026, session 1112)

The rows of ISO-NE, NYISO and MISO in the tables the status file holds by grid (`site/data/license_status.json`; the owner's ruling of 12 October 2026) are never read for an answer. A query that asks for a held grid's rows, by its entity or, with none, by the chat's own grid, returns no figure of it: the grid named, "its rows are held for a license: license needed", the hold's words, and the same query's figure for each public grid of the table (one read by entity), so the answer can say what the warehouse does hold in the same sentence: "NYISO's rows are held for a license (license needed). For the public grids, the flat price in July 2026: ERCOT 29.17 USD/MWh, CAISO 39.43 USD/MWh, SPP 53.79 USD/MWh (merchant_revenue_monthly)." The hold itself is unchanged by this. MISO's rows are also paused, and its pause says more than its license: a question of MISO's rows, by its entity or in MISO's scoped chat, is refused with the pause's words (23 October 2026, session 1126; session 1113's run found MISO's chat answered the held grid's way). A `compare` with a held grid's side returns both sides and makes no difference or ratio with the held one (session 1126; it crashed the loop before).

## What an answer shows (23 October 2026, session 1115)

Under the answer and its sources, one line for each query or compare it read: the table, the entity, the variable, the window (the end not included) and the aggregation, never a figure; and how long it took, with how many reads of the warehouse.

## A conversation (23 October 2026, session 1129)

The general chat and a grid's scoped chat hold the last three turns, as Ask ERCOT has since session 121: each turn's question, its answer (to 1,500 characters) and what its queries read go into the first message before the new question, in Ask ERCOT's own words; an earlier answer's numbers count as given (they were checked when it was written) and its cited tables as read; every new number is still fetched and checked. With no earlier turn the first message is what it was (`site/lib/chat/history.ts`).

## A chart under an answer (23 October 2026, session 1142)

An answer of the general chat that rests on a series carries it, as Ask ERCOT's has since session 92: the last result grouped by time (year, month, day or hour) of a table the answer cites, at least three rows, its rows the tool's own, key by key and value by value (`site/lib/chat/generalseries.ts`, checked by `series.ts`). An answer that cites no such result carries none.

The model is told which table each economics page reads (rule 9 of the system prompt): the battery page `battery_stack_monthly`, the generator page `merchant_revenue_monthly` (whose battery rows are the generator page's rule for a battery), the datacenter page `cost_of_power_monthly`. ## The Python loop (23 October 2026, session 1146)

The CLI's and the evaluation's own loop (`warehouse/chat/ask.py`) holds the conversation, records its reads and names a refusal's nearest tables as the site's does; the license hold stays the site's.

## The license hold in the Python loop (23 October 2026, session 1191)

## A table the live set lacks (13 November 2026, session 5256)

The catalogue lists every table of the record; the live set this chat reads holds some of them (`in_live_set` yes or review). Asked for a figure of a table the live set lacks, the chat cannot read it, and the answer is a refusal, never an answer:

- **The tool's refusal says where the table is and how to answer**, as a held table's does: "table X is not in this site's live set; its full history is on Redivis. No tool here can read it, and no figure of it may be stated. Another table you can read may hold what is asked (a derived table often carries a source table's figures): look before you refuse. If none does, answer with not_in_warehouse true and say that X is on Redivis and not in the live set this chat reads" (`site/lib/chat/refused.ts` `notLiveWords`, `warehouse/chat/tools.py` `not_live_words`: one sentence; the Python tools say it when their backend is the live set alone, `live_only`). The middle sentence is session 5258's: the first wording said only how to refuse, and the held-out nu08, refused EIA's generator table, found the unit's nameplate in `energy_projects` all the same. The words must not forbid the search rule 3 asks for.
- **A refused table is no source of an answer.** The loop counts every table `list_tables` names as read, so that what the catalogue says of a table can be cited. Since this session an answer's citation of a table a tool refused to read (not in the live set, held for a license, MISO's pause), and that no call read, is a citation of a table no tool read: the draft is sent back once, as for any cited table never read. ## A window says whether it is whole, and the chat is told to stop (14 November 2026, sessions 5266 to 5268)

- **Why.** Of the 463 tool calls the platform set's 80 figure answers made on 13 November, 257 came after a result already held the figure (`warehouse/chat/eval/calls_after.py`; the classes are in `docs/reviews/ask-eval-2026-11-13.md`). The figure was in hand by the fourth call every time; twelve answers ran to the limit of 12. The chat's ten rules said nothing about stopping, and the tool did not say whether a window was whole, so the chat counted the rows and grouped them again to find out.
- **The window field** (`site/lib/chat/window.ts`, `warehouse/chat/window.py`; the same function in both loops). A query of a series with both `start` and `end` returns `window`: the window read (`from`, `until`), the table's `step`, `steps_held`, `steps_in_window`, `steps_missing`, `whole` (true, false, or null when it cannot be told), the first and last step held, and the first six runs of missing steps (`missing`, each with `from`, `until` and `steps`). With several series matched it gives the least and the most any one holds. With no row matched and one series named, it says so: 0 of the steps the window has.
- **Made from what is already read.** The rows the query read and the table's step. The Python loop takes the step from the rows' own `freq`; the site's loop reads no `freq`, so it takes the steps the catalogue lists for the table, and of several the smallest gap between two neighbouring rows when that gap is one of them. No request is added and none is changed, so the recorded reads of the stored tests are found as before. Where the step cannot be told (a table of two steps and one row; a row off the step's grid), `whole` is null and the note says so: it is never whole and never in part on a guess.
- **A local day is counted in instants.** The window is the two instants the rows were bounded by, so the day the clocks go back has 25 hourly steps and the day they go forward 23.
- **What a whole window does not say.** On a table of days, weeks, months or years a whole window means every row is held. A month's row may still cover 48 of its 744 hours, and such a table says so in a variable of its own (`da_hours`, `days_held`). The note of a whole window of such a table says that, and rule 11 keeps that one check.
- **Rule 11, to stop.** When a result holds the figure, answer. The same rows asked again with another grouping, time zone, aggregation or filter are not a second source. Whether a window is complete is in `window`; when it is not whole, the answer says which part is held and gives a figure only as the figure of the steps held. A further call is for something that can be named: a figure the results do not hold, a second figure the question asks for, the hours or days behind a row of a table of days or longer.
- **Not changed:** Ask ERCOT's own rules and its fast path (`warehouse/chat/ercot.py`, `spec_ercot.json`); the limit of 12 calls; the forms "newest day" and "this week", which carry their own count of days.
- **Held by** `tests/test_session5267_ask.py` and `site/scripts/test-ask-window.test.mjs` (35 made windows answered alike by both functions; the Python tool on session 1300's rows), `tests/test_session5268_ask.py` (the rule, the description, the exported spec), and the stored replays of sessions 143, 156 and 161, which set the new key apart and check it on their recorded rows (four Central days of hourly demand: 96 of 96).

## The evaluation's spend guard reads the whole ledger (14 November 2026, session 5263)

- **What it is.** The asking through the site's route (`eval_s1113.py --ask`, `run_eval.py`) checks before each question that the day's spend of the evaluation's step, read from the site's cost ledger, and a reserve stay under `--cap` (`spent_today`).
- **The fault** (found by session 5262). It asked for the day's rows in one request with `limit=5000`. Supabase answers a request with at most 1,000 rows whatever limit it names. On 13 November 2026 the day held 1,092 rows and the function counted 1,000: USD 18.01 of USD 19.46. No cap was passed that day: every asking went through the site's loop, whose ceiling is counted on its own run file, and the run files and the whole ledger agree to the cent.
- **The reading now.** A page at a time in the order of the ledger's key, each page beginning after the last row read, until a page comes back empty, so it does not lean on the size of the server's page and a row written meanwhile is counted once. The server's exact count is then held against the rows read.
- **A day that cannot be counted stops the asking.** Fewer rows read than the server counts, a page that does not move on, an answer that stops part way, a call with no price: no call is made (`LedgerNotWhole`). Before, a call with no price fell back to the run's own costs. A ledger that cannot be asked at all (no keys on the machine, no answer) still falls back to the run's own costs, and a line says so.
- **Two commands.** `eval_s1113.py --spent` prints the day's spend from the whole ledger (reads only; exit 3 when the day cannot be counted, 4 when the ledger cannot be asked). `eval_site.py --day-cap <USD>` holds every asking of the UTC day that writes the ledger under one ceiling: the day is read first, the session's ceiling is cut to what the day has left, and nothing is asked when the day cannot be counted. It goes without `--no-ledger`.
- **No other spend guard reads its total through one request of rows:** the site's ceilings are sums the database makes (`site_ask_admit`, `internal_ask_spend`), `warehouse/llm.py` reads its ledger file whole, the thesis runner sums in SQL, and the daily cap of the scheduled steps reads Supabase's copy in pages (`warehouse/health.py` `ledger_remote`).
- **Held by** `tests/test_session5263_ask.py`: a stand-in server that holds 1,092 rows and answers 1,000 at most.

## A repeated call, and a figure as returned (6 November 2026, session 2222)
