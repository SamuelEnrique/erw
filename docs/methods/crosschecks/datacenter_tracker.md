# Cross-checks: the datacenter power tracker (`/datacenters`)

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW). Session 7403, chain 303, 16 November 2026. The done line's cross-check for the tracker: **its totals beside the figures a reader at a lab or a fund would know**, each with its publisher, document and date, and why the two differ. No figure of the tracker was adjusted to match one.

- **Ours** is `replication/datacenter_tracker/page_figures.json`: the live set's 458 rows of `datacenter_facilities` as read on 16 November 2026 at 09:18 UTC (vintage 15 November), by the page's own functions, which the replication reproduces 454 of 454. `tests/test_session7403_trackers.py` holds each "Ours" below to that file.
- **Theirs** was read by this session from the publisher's own document on 16 November 2026 between 09:40 and 09:43 UTC, each host's robots file first. Every figure is stated as a fact with a link to its document; no publisher's text is quoted (the owner's ruling of 14 October 2026 for statements on large loads, kept here for every row).
- **What was not read.** MISO: paused since 4 October 2026 and never requested (`warehouse/metadata/paused_sources.csv`). The SEC's EDGAR: it asks an automated reader for a contact address, which this session does not send, so the utilities' figures are from their own sites and from PJM's committee pages. AEP's and Dominion's news pages are drawn by script and gave no text; their figures are from the release and the presentation as files. The warehouse's own table of such statements, `large_load_statements` (598 megawatt figures of 113 utilities and grid bodies), is internal and not on this machine; nothing here is from it.
- **The columns.** "Gap" is ours less theirs, in the same unit, where the two measure the same kind of thing; where they do not, the cell says so and the reason says what each counts.

## What the table says of the tracker

- **It is a list of named sites, not a measure of megawatts.** 253 of its 274 counted facilities state no megawatts, and five rows carry 25,000 of the 28,254 MW stated.
- **Against every published total it is small**, and for stated reasons: the publishers count load under contract or in a queue without naming it; the tracker counts only what a source names and sizes.
- **Where it can be checked row by row, it was**: the largest megawatt rows against their stories' own words (faults rows 18 to 21 of `docs/reviews/datacenter-faults-2026-11-11.md`). Three of the fourteen largest carry a size that is not the facility's own (a tenant named as operator is a fourth).
- **ERCOT's figures on the page are ERCOT's**: the 38 figures of the twelve reports are in the replication, each equal to the live set's row.
