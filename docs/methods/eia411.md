# EIA-411 peak load, capacity resources and capacity margins by NERC assessment area

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), method note written in session 905 (chain 049) for tables first written on 23 October 2026.

Tables: `eia411_capacity_margins_annual`

## Publisher and address

U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Form EIA-411, Coordinated Bulk Power Supply and Demand Program Report, the
program's last release (November 2016), page `https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia411/`. Three workbooks under
`xls/`, each read from its sheet `Sheet1`:

- `peak_load_2016.xls`, Table 2: noncoincident peak load, summer and winter (MW).
- `summer_capacity_2016.xls`, Table 4.A: summer net internal demand and capacity resources (MW), capacity margin (percent).
- `winter_capacity_2016.xls`, Table 4.B: the same for winter.

All three retrieved 2026-10-23T06:13:11Z and saved in `warehouse/raw/eia411/` (not in git), reused with `--offline`.
Connector: `warehouse/connectors/eia411.py`. Ceiling three requests a run (the three files). EIA's robots file allows
`/electricity/data/` (it disallows `/archive/`).

Session 895 took this program because the Electric Power Annual, which the queue file named, no longer carries a NERC
peak demand or capacity margin table; EIA-411's page still links its last release.

## Vintage and revision

One release, dated: actual years 1990 to 2016 and projected years 2017 to 2027 (EIA's "E" years), written by run
`20261023T061506Z`. A run reads the three files and replaces the table whole. `vintage` is empty on every row; the
release is named in the header.

The areas change over time, as the header says: ECAR, MAAC and MAIN dissolved at the end of 2005 into RFC, and MAPP was
absorbed into MISO, SPP and WECC in 2015. EIA's Balance of Eastern Region gives a consistent eastern trend. EIA notes
that capacity margins before 2015 are its own rough calculation from the areas' figures, and from 2015 NERC's, and that
the Balance of Eastern Region and the contiguous U.S. are computed by EIA, not precise. All are kept as given.

## Shape and variables

Series. `entity` `eia411:<assessment area>`, the area's name as EIA writes it, in lower case with every run of other
characters made `_`. `ts_utc` the peak period's first day as EIA defines it: summer 1 June of the year, winter 1 October
of the season's first year (the winter of 1990/1991 is `1990-10-01`; EIA's winter runs October to May). `freq` `P1Y`,
`geo` `US`, `market` empty, `node` the area and its interconnection as EIA gives them (for example `TRE (ERCOT)`,
`WECC (Western Interconnection)`). EIA's own field: `x_projected`, `actual` or `projected`.

| Variable | Unit | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `summer_peak_load`, `winter_peak_load` | `MW` | noncoincident peak load (Table 2) |
| `summer_net_internal_demand`, `winter_net_internal_demand` | `MW` | net internal demand (Tables 4.A and 4.B) |
| `summer_capacity_resources`, `winter_capacity_resources` | `MW` | capacity resources |
| `summer_capacity_margin`, `winter_capacity_margin` | `pct` | unused capability at peak as a share of capacity resources, as EIA's heading names it (but see the cross-check) |

The 16 entities: `frcc`, `npcc`, `serc`, `spp`, `tre`, `wecc` and EIA's `balance_of_eastern_region` and
`contiguous_u_s`, 1990 to 2027; `mro` 1990 to 2009; `ecar`, `maac` and `main` 1990 to 2004; `rfc` 2005 to
2009; `mapp` 2010 to 2014; `miso` and `pjm` 2010 to 2027.

When written: 2,924 rows (2,226 in MW, 698 in percent). Summer: each of the four variables 305 actual rows (1990 to
2016) and 110 projected (2017 to 2027). Winter peak load the same; winter net internal demand, capacity resources and
capacity margin 173 actual rows, from the winter of 2001/2002, and 110 projected. Contiguous U.S.: summer peak load
546,331 MW in 1990 and 768,510 MW in 2016; summer capacity margin 21.6 percent in 1990 and 16.8 percent in 2016.

## Reading the sheets

- A block is found by its heading row: "Interconnection" in the first column, "NERC Regional" at the start of the second
  (EIA writes "NERC Regional Assesment Area"), and the measure in the third. The measure is matched by its first words
  (net internal demand, capacity resources, capacity margin, and summer or winter for the peak load blocks); a measure
  the map does not know stops the run.
- The years are on the row two below the heading row. A winter season split over two rows ("1990/" above "1991") is
  joined; a year ending in "E" is projected. A year label of any other form stops the run.
- The areas run down from the row after the years until the second column is empty or a new heading row begins. The
  interconnection in the first column carries down until another is named.
- "NA" or an empty cell has no row. Nothing is filled.
- An entity, variable and date given twice stops the run.
- A footnote mark on an area's name is not part of it. The winter capacity file names RFC `RFC[5]`; the first write
  (session 895) kept the mark, so RFC's winter figures for 2005 to 2009 stood under a second entity, `eia411:rfc_5`, and
  the header counted 17 areas. This note's check found it; session 905 strips the mark and wrote the table again: RFC is
  one entity and the header counts 16 areas.
- Each run checks every margin against its own row's resources and demand and names in the header the margins that equal
  (resources less demand) over demand, a reserve margin, rather than over resources. They are kept as EIA gives them.

## Cross-check (session 895)

Each margin against its own row's resources and demand:

- Before 2015, when EIA calculated the margins: 284 of 285 summer rows and 151 of 153 winter rows equal (resources less
  demand) over resources, the capacity margin EIA's heading names. Recomputed from the table in session 905, the three
  that do not are FRCC's summer 1990 and PJM's winters 2010 and 2011.
- From 2015, when NERC sent them: 75 of 130 a season match that, and 55 instead equal (resources less demand) over
  demand, a reserve margin. They are FRCC, MISO, PJM, SPP and TRE, every projected year 2017 to 2027 in both seasons
  (110 values), named in the header.
- ERCOT's own CDR margins (`ercot_cdr_reserve_margins`) start in 2026 and do not overlap. EIA-930's demand table holds no
  2015 or 2016 hours, so the peak loads could not be checked against it.

## What it does not hold

- Actual figures after 2016: the release ends there, and its projections end in 2027. NERC's current assessments are not
  read (whether to read NERC's Long-Term Reliability Assessment is left to Samuel's ruling).
- Winter net internal demand, capacity resources and capacity margin before the winter of 2001/2002.
- A margin made consistent: the 110 reserve margins stay as EIA gives them, under the capacity margin's name.
- Anything below the assessment area: no utility, balancing authority or state.
- EIA's footnotes, other than the marks left in an area's name.
