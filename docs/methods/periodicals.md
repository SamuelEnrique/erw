# The Daily Digest and the Weekly Roundup: how each part is chosen

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW). The ERW's two periodicals, the Daily Digest (weekdays) and the Weekly Roundup (Sundays), in the v2 shape the owner ruled on 24 October 2026 from the brandbook's pages (`docs/brand.md`, section 10). This note says how each part is chosen, which rule holds it and under which ruling (`docs/reviews/periodicals-rulings-2026-10-23.md`, items 1 to 19, applied as defaults under the owner's 24-hour rule by sessions 2701 and 2702, then read as a sharp reader reads them by chain 149, sessions 1433 to 1456, `docs/reviews/periodicals-faults-2026-11-05.md`), which key traces each figure, and when and to whom each issue is sent. Written by session 1452 (6 November 2026). The code: `warehouse/news/brief.py` (the Digest), `warehouse/news/roundup.py` (the Roundup), `warehouse/news/email_digest.py` (the emails), `warehouse/news/score.py` with `warehouse/news/rubric.md` (the scoring), `warehouse/analysis/findings/daily_chart.py` (the chart of the day), `warehouse/news/funfact.py` (the fun fact), `site/lib/digest.ts` and `site/lib/markdown.ts` (the pages); the tracers `scripts/check_digest.py` and `scripts/check_roundup.py`.

## The stories

- **The window.** A Digest covers the stories from the previous issue's end to its cutoff, capped at 96 hours (item 3; `brief.window_start`, `WINDOW_CAP_HOURS`), so a missed weekday is caught up and Friday evening is in Monday's issue; a story dated after the run is left for the next issue (session 2206). The masthead says the cutoff in Eastern Time with UTC; when the newest story held is more than two hours before it, the masthead says "Stories through ...; prices through ..." (session 1444).
- **The scoring.** A model scores every story against the rubric (`rubric.md`): significance across the whole energy industry, an AI relevance tag that earns no credit by itself, a sector, a region, a capacity in MW only when the text states one in MW, GW or kW (an energy unit, GWh, is refused: session 1446), a price as written, the parties, a headline, and one line on the consequence: one sentence, no adjective of the list (massive, unprecedented, significant, major, critical, key, huge), no verb that asserts nothing (signals, demonstrates, reflects, underscores, highlights), one fact the story gives that the title does not, no figure the title already gives (sessions 801 and 1437).
- **The why line's check.** The brief holds the scored line to the same rule (`brief.why_check`): an adjective, a hollow verb, a second sentence, a figure the headline holds, or a line whose content words are half or more the headline's (`brief.restates`, 0.5) fails; so does a line that names no party, place, date, quantity or rule the headline lacks (`brief.new_things`) when it shares a quarter of its content words with the headline or strings two clauses on a semicolon (session 1458, fault 7: 100 of the 324 lines the 13 stored issues printed would fail it); a failing line prints nothing and the item carries the headline alone (item 16; nothing invented, nothing said twice).
- **One event, one item; one topic, one item.** Clusters of one event are one (session 470). In the top ten a topic is the sector with the region (item 13, `brief.same_topic`); two price stories of one commodity are one topic whatever their regions (session 1438, `brief.price_commodity`); a sector holds at most two items (`SECTOR_MAX`); the rest of a topic folds under its item as "Also on this topic" lines (at most three), which do not count toward the fold (item 14).
- **The ranking.** By significance; at one score an event before commentary (a column, an interview, a question: session 814's `kind`), then the number of outlets, then the time; AI relevance is no key (item 4). A title about oil or gas prices or futures is of that commodity's sector whatever power tag the scorer gave (session 1443, `brief.sector_of`).
- **The sources.** The link text is the feed's own outlet name; a Google News address is said so, "(via Google News)"; a held outlet keeps its link (item 15); a cluster's links reach the outlet's own page before Google's (session 1442, `brief.direct_first`). The resolver that would reach the outlet behind a Google address is the data machine's and waits for a ruling.

## The Daily Digest, part by part

1. **The masthead**: "ERW Daily Digest", the date in words, the cutoff ("Data through 11:45 ET, 15:45 UTC", never "real-time"), the subject line as a comment (`ERW Daily | <metric>: <value and unit>, <comparison period>`, a figure only when it is a warehouse table's), and the opening number's key for the next issue's novelty.
2. **The opening number** (`brief.opening_number`): among the day's moves (the main hubs' day-ahead means and EIA's spot closes, each against the value before it, within the cutoff: session 1439) and the day's top story's figure, by consequence first (a move a story of the day names, `brief.price_named`; a benchmark before a hub), novelty second (yesterday's metric again only at twice the bar), source quality third; a move needs its bar (a benchmark 3 percent, a hub 10 percent), 1.5 times that when no story names it (session 1440); a hub no story names opens the issue only when no named move, no unnamed benchmark past its bar and no top story's structural figure exists; never the largest percentage move by default. The key is the mover's (`digest_fuel`, `digest_dam`).
3. **The numbers block** (`brief.numbers_block`): four rows. A market price (the best candidate the opening number did not take, `digest_fuel` or `digest_dam`, both values keyed); a physical measure (US48 demand's peak hour against the same weekday a year before, or a week before while the year is not held and said so: item 18, session 1439; `digest_demand`, both peaks keyed); a project or capacity figure and a policy, capital or reliability figure from the day's stories in rank (the whole issue's items), each in the block's form, the measure named by the sector, the place by the region, the figure once, who and the outlet, "first report" and "the outlet's figure, not a table's", keyed to the story's scored row (`digest_story|news_stories|<cluster id>|mw` or `|price`, session 1450); a price story is never the policy row. A held or restricted value prints its status, never a digit (item 1). A revised figure is a note on the page, not a fault (item 7 of the deals rulings; session 2207).
4. **What moved** (`brief.middle`): the top of the top ten after one topic one item, each with its headline, its why line when it passed the check, one limitation when material (an analysis piece; one outlet so far), its sources; as many stories as bring the prose above the fold to 350 words, at most five, two when three stand past 450 (session 1444, `brief.lift_count`). The rest of the top ten, the sectors, AI and power and the numbers in full fold under "More" (item 5: fold, do not cut).
5. **The chart of the day** (`daily_chart.choose`): the candidates most related to the day's top stories first (session 805, `relevance`), then the scanner's rule (records and first negatives, then strength), then the watch list; a candidate whose series name is an id, or whose strength is a step from zero (past 50 robust standard deviations), is never the chart (session 1441); a flag under 1.5 robust standard deviations (item 6b) or whose draft was dismissed (6c) is passed over; the source line carries publisher, table, period, vintage and the ERW's retrieval time; when no candidate passes, a small table of the day's movers stands in and the issue says no chart is shown. The caption's figures are keyed (`digest_chart`); on the page a hover gives the chart's figures.
6. **The fun fact** (`funfact.py`): one, from the bank, re-verified against its stored source when the issue is written (a failure gives no section); a pun holds no number and, when it states a claim, its own verified span (item 7); a warehouse fact's numbers carry the issue's thousands separator and link their table (session 1448). The 14 October issue stays as written with one line on the page (item 8).
7. **The footer**: methods, corrections, preferences, unsubscribe.

The emails (`email_digest.render`): the same order; one "Tables" line for the opening number and the block, after the block, small; a table name wraps at 390 px; the brand's fonts first with their fallbacks; no script, no stylesheet; the character (ERW, direction 5) in the fun-fact module only, alt "ERW"; the preview path (`--preview`) sends to the fixed recipients only. On the page each `[n]` mark is a hover naming its table and the Tables line sits closed under "Sources" (session 1449).

## The Weekly Roundup, part by part

1. **The masthead**: "ERW Weekly Roundup", the week ending date, the sources' cutoff in Eastern Time with UTC, one line when the previous ISO week has no issue (item 10).
2. **The opening number** (`roundup.opening_number`): a benchmark's close a week apart that moved 3 percent or more and that a story of the week names; else the deal of the week at USD 1 billion or more; else the largest datacenter load first reported at 500 MW or more; else the top story's structural figure; else an unnamed benchmark at 4.5 percent; else a hub's weekly mean at 10 percent, last; else the US48 peak; its status said (observed, calculated, reported) (session 1446).
3. **The week in five numbers** (`roundup.five_numbers`): the week's largest price move (`rdp_five_dam`, `rdp_five_fuel`), the US48 peak with its hour (`rdp_five_us48`), the largest deal by US dollars (`rdp_five_deal`), the largest new datacenter load with an operator or a site, the chart's figure with whose week it is; a category with nothing stands in with the next price move, said so (session 1446). On the email and the page a row that is the opening number word for word reads "the opening number, above".
4. **What mattered**: three developments by consequence, each with its why line when it passed, what the evidence does not establish from the record alone (a figure the headline states is the outlet's; no party named; no capacity or sum stated; one outlet; an analysis piece; nothing when every field is stated: session 1446), its sources.
5. **The chart of the week**: the data machine's rule's pick with the engine's note; one sentence naming the measure the week's stories point to when it is another (item 17 stays for the data machine).
6. **Next week** (`roundup.next_week`): dated items the policy monitor holds (comment deadlines, effective dates), titles cut at a word with the docket's short name, one item of a kind from one agency a day (session 1447); "no dated events held this week" when none.
7. **The fun fact**, as the Digest's; the character here only.
8. **More**: the weekend's and the week's other stories; the deals and datacenter tables with confidence 0.7 or more and at least one of operator, site or MW, US rows first, at most 15 a section with the count of the rest and a link (item 11); the policy of the week; the numbers in full, whose summary has one number a clause (session 1447). The coverage charts stay excluded (item 9); a no-model headline keeps the publisher's words, said so (item 12). The weekend line says no story of those days is scored when the scorer did not run (session 1447).

## The tracers

`scripts/check_digest.py` traces every keyed figure of every Digest to the warehouse (the live set, then the CSVs): `digest_dam`, `digest_rt`, `digest_fuel`, `digest_demand`, `digest_story`, `digest_chart`, and the fun fact's stored sources; a figure the publisher revised after an issue written inside the publisher's revision window of its period is "revised", not failed (sessions 2206 and 1450). `scripts/check_roundup.py` traces the five numbers and the numbers section by their forms (`rdp_*`). `site/scripts/check-values.mjs` reads each page's keyed figures against the live set and passes a revised figure as a note at the live value; the landing gate counts the notes. A figure with no key is a fault.

## When and to whom

- The Digest is written and sent by the daily run (`.github/workflows/daily-prices.yml`, started at 14:00 UTC by pg_cron, migration 015), weekdays; the brandbook's recommended send is 09:00 Eastern with an 08:30 Eastern cutoff: open, the owner's (the workflow's `schedule` line and the pg_cron job).
- The Roundup is written and sent on Sundays at 23:00 UTC (`.github/workflows/roundup.yml`, migration 022); the brandbook's recommendation is Fridays 10:00 Eastern with a Thursday 18:00 Eastern cutoff: open, the owner's.
- The recipients are the fixed `DIGEST_RECIPIENTS` only while the news index's license is held (item 2); subscribers are read only with `EMAIL_SUBSCRIBERS=1`.
- A dry run (`--out`, `--now`) writes to its own path and never a past issue; a past issue is a record and is never rewritten (item 8); a backfill is the owner's.

## Changed on 13 November 2026

Chain 228 read the Digest of 12 November and the Roundups of W45 and W46 as a reader (faults file rows 79 to 99). For a reader of the issues:

- The subject line of both periodicals carries the top story's headline with no figure when the opening is not a warehouse table's figure.
- The Digest's opening says which single hub's move it passed over, instead of saying no price moved; its market row is never a price that did not move; "What moved most" lists the prices that moved and names the unchanged ones on one line.
- An EIA close is dated by its trading day; the lag is said to run from one day to about a week.
- Outlets are named by their names ("JPT"), and a party the headline names is not printed again.
- The chart of the day says which story it follows only when that story names its measure, and a record's "record until now" is the highest value before it.
- The Roundup's weekly table prints "licensed source needed" for a grid held for license, as the Digest does, and its page holds the figures the stored issues of W44 and W45 printed for NYISO and ISO-NE.
- The Roundup's deal of the week names the outlet of its sum; a stand-in price move passes the opening's own bar; a story naming a benchmark is given the warehouse's close.

Later the same day (rows 100 to 130): a Roundup opens on the week's benchmark move when any story of the week explains it, with that story's reason; a reason or a pointer is never a story of the other direction, of another gas market, or one quoting a price of its own; the five numbers say what they leave out; each list is numbered from 1; the Digest's first line says when a window holds few stories or many unscored ones; and no en dash or publisher's tail is printed from a feed's title.
