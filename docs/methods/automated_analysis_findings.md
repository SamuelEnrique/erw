# Automated Analysis findings: method

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), session 170. Automated Analysis (platform tool 26) produces findings on demand from the warehouse, as cards modeled on the peak premium panel of the owner's Energy Intelligence thesis ([`ercot_peak_premium.md`](ercot_peak_premium.md)). This note is the method of the engine and of the first three findings; the words on a card are the ERW's own ([`docs/voice.md`](../voice.md), "The finding card").

## The engine

- A finding is a module in `warehouse/analysis/findings/` that declares its id, its title, its kind (`visual`: a trend, map or distribution; `econometric`: an event study, a before-and-after with controls, or a fixed-effects regression with standard errors, shown as a table with the effect stated in plain words), its inputs (each with a default and its choices, in human words) and the tables it reads.
- `compute(params, in_dir)` reads the tables from the warehouse's output directory and returns the finding's data rows and what the rows alone cannot say; `card(rows, params, meta)` builds the card, and `numbers_from_rows(rows)` recomputes every number on it from the rows alone. A test reads each card's CSV download back and proves the card from it (`tests/test_session170.py`); the full recomputation against the tables runs with `ERW_FINDINGS_FULL=1`.
- Each card downloads its data (`study_year_topic_word.csv`), the Python that computed it (the module as run) and a Stata do-file to the owner's rules: no line continuation operators; one empty line between commands outside loops and none inside; every variable destrung with force; per-variable sentinel recoding before any reshape loop.
- `run_finding.py <finding> --param k=v` writes the card to `site/data/findings/` and the downloads to `site/public/findings/`; a card with chosen inputs is named `<finding>__<input>-<value>`, so the default card stands. A table that is not on the machine is said; nothing is filled in.
- **The queue.** On `/analysis` a person picks a finding and its inputs (no typed free text). The request is a row of `public.analysis_requests` (migration 027, applied to the project on 10 October 2026 by session 172; internal: row level security on, no policy, the public key reads and writes nothing; the site writes through a function behind the internal token, at most 24 run requests a UTC day). The data machine, the laptop that holds the full histories, runs `warehouse/analysis/findings/worker.py --loop 60`: it takes the oldest queued row, marks it running with its name, runs the finding and writes the card into the row (done, or failed with the plain reason). The card appears on the page in 1 to 5 minutes when the machine is awake. **When the machine is off the row stays queued**: the page shows it with the time it was asked, and the worker takes it when the machine wakes. The worker never takes the data lock: a finding reads tables and writes no warehouse table.
- **The Roundup.** "Use in Roundup" on a card writes a row of kind `roundup` for the week. The Sunday Roundup (`warehouse/news/roundup.py`) takes the latest such row for its week and prints the card's words; none chosen, it prints the rule's chart of the week and says "the rule's pick; no finding was chosen for this week". `roundup.yml` restores the findings' tables on the runner (`run_finding.py --tables`) beside the watch list's; a table it cannot restore is said and the Roundup goes on.
- **Renders.** `site/scripts/render-cards.mjs` photographs each card at 1080 x 1350 (LinkedIn) and 1600 x 900 (X) from the built site in a real browser, in Source Serif 4 (title and subtitle) and Inter (the rest), bundled under the SIL Open Font License from `site/public/fonts/` with their license files. The renderer fails on any font fallback: a face not loaded from the bundle, or a title laid out in another face, is exit 1 and no PNG.

## Finding 1: "Batteries ate their own lunch?" (econometric)

- **Data.** ERCOT real-time settlement point prices at the chosen hub (default HB_HUBAVG, the thesis's hub), 15-minute intervals, from the first year (default 2019) to the latest interval: `ercot_all_hub_prices_history` (NP6-785-ER) then `iso_rtm_hub_prices` (NP6-905-CD) for the live window after it, each interval once. Periods are the local (America/Chicago) month and year, as the peak premium method cuts them.
- **Spike measures, per month and per year.** The median and the 99.9th percentile (numpy's linear percentile, no cap or exclusion), their ratio (the worst-interval multiple), hours at or above USD 1,000/MWh and USD 250/MWh (intervals over four), and hours at or above the 99th percentile of every interval in the sample (one fixed threshold, named on the card).
- **The fleet.** `storage_buildout_monthly`, `iso:ercot` `battery_operating_mw` (EIA-860M operating units, by the month of the data). **Load:** `ercot_zone_load_hourly`, the ERCOT total row (else the sum of the zones each hour), monthly mean MW. **Gas:** `eia_fuel_spot_prices`, Henry Hub daily spot, monthly mean.
- **The regression.** OLS of each monthly spike measure on battery GW, mean load in GW, Henry Hub in USD/MMBtu and eleven month-of-year dummies, over the months that hold every regressor (months after the latest fleet figure are left out). HC1 standard errors (Stata's `vce(robust)`), p from Student's t on n minus k degrees of freedom. The do-file runs the same regressions.
- **Causal language.** A before-and-after with controls: the card says "goes with" and "association", and names what the design cannot separate (the 2021 winter storm, the offer cap, solar, the gas price). The subtitle is chosen by the fit: negative and p below 0.05, positive and p below 0.05, or flat, each in its own words.

## Finding 2: "Gas sets the price less often?" (visual)

- **Statistic.** For each grid and local year from 2019, the percent of held hours in which the hourly real-time price was below a gas plant's fuel cost, heat rate times Henry Hub: 7.0 MMBtu/MWh on the card, 6.5 and 8.0 in the chart's hover and the CSV. Henry Hub is the calendar month's mean of EIA's daily spot, applied to every hour of the month (no day is filled). Henry Hub is the national benchmark; New England's gas is dearer at Algonquin, so ISO-NE's share reads low, and the card says so.
- **Hourly prices.** ERCOT: HB_HUBAVG, the mean of the four 15-minute prices in the hour (history and live window). ISO-NE: the simple mean of the eight load zones' hourly real-time LMP from `isone_zone_prices_history` (an internal table; only these yearly aggregates are published). CAISO: TH_SP15_GEN-APND; SPP: SPPSOUTH_HUB; NYISO: the simple mean of the eleven zones; each the mean of the 15-minute means in the hour, held in the live tables from 2026-08-26 (SPP from 2026-09-24). Hours are floored in UTC and labeled in the grid's local zone.
- **Not held.** A year a grid's history does not cover is blank in the CSV and "not held" on the card; the 2026 bars of CAISO, NYISO and SPP cover weeks, and the card says which. MISO is paused ("paused while terms are reviewed") and PJM licensed ("licensed source needed"): placeholders in the site's greyed style.

## Finding 4: "The peak hour moved" (visual, session 174)

- **Question.** Has the hour of the day's highest real-time price moved as solar grew, in ERCOT and CAISO, 2019 to 2026?
- **Data.** ERCOT: HB_HUBAVG real-time settlement point prices, 15-minute, `ercot_all_hub_prices_history` (2019 to 2026-08-26) and `iso_rtm_hub_prices` (after). CAISO: TH_SP15_GEN-APND real-time, 15-minute, `iso_hub_prices_history` (from 2024-09-01, the first CAISO prices the warehouse holds) and `iso_rtm_hub_prices`. Solar: `eia860m_operating_generators` and `eia860m_retired_generators_all`, Solar Photovoltaic units with the balancing authority code ERCO or CISO, nameplate MW.
- **Method.** Each local clock hour's price is the mean of its intervals; a day's peak hour is the hour with the highest mean (the first on a tie); a day needs at least 20 held hours (input: 20 or 24). Per grid and year: the percent of days peaking in each hour of the day, the median peak hour, the percent peaking 17:00 to 21:59 (evening) and 10:00 to 15:59 (midday), the modal hour. Solar at year end: units operating today by their operating year, plus units retired since by their retirement year, as EIA-860M's August 2026 inventory records them (units retired before the retired list begins are not counted). A year a grid's prices do not cover is not held; CAISO's partial first year (2024, from September) is a row but not a chart line.
- **Chart.** Lines over the 24 hours: the percent of the year's days peaking in each hour, ERCOT's first and last full year, CAISO's full years and the current year to date.

## Finding 5: "Who rescues whom" (visual, session 174)

- **Question.** During Winter Storm Uri, Winter Storm Elliott, the summer 2023 heat in ERCOT and the August 2020 heat in CAISO, which balancing authorities flipped from net importing to net exporting, and which the other way?
- **Data.** `eia930_daily_interchange` (EIA-930's daily interchange by pair, 2019 on, EIA's Eastern-time days); the windows from `event_window_daily`'s header (docs/methods/events.md).
- **Method.** EIA's region rows (CAL, CAR, CENT, FLA, MIDA, MIDW, NE, NW, NY, SE, SW, TEN, TEX, US48) are left out. EIA reports each pair twice, once by each side, and the sides disagree at times (on 2021-02-15 PJM reported 432,663 MWh sent to MISO and MISO reported 178,851 MWh received from PJM): a pair's flow on a day is the mean of the two sides' reports where both are held, and the footnote counts the pair-days whose sides differ by more than a tenth. An authority's day is the sum over its pairs, exports minus imports. The window mean is set against the baseline mean, the same calendar days 364 and 728 days earlier. A flip is a change of sign; an authority under the smallest flow counted (input: 0, 1,000 or 5,000 MWh a day) on both sides is "small". Every authority with days on both sides is a row of the CSV; the chart shows the flipped ones and the event's grid, largest swing first, at most 14.
- **What it does not say.** Why a sign moved: a tie-line limit, a neighbour's own emergency or an outage can each do it, and EIA's interchange data carry known reporting issues.

## Finding 6: "Negative prices march west" (visual, session 174)

- **Question.** How often do the ERCOT hubs clear below zero, West against Houston (with North and South), by year 2019 to 2026, and SPP's North hub for the years held?
- **Data.** ERCOT: HB_WEST, HB_HOUSTON, HB_NORTH, HB_SOUTH real-time settlement point prices, 15-minute, `ercot_all_hub_prices_history` and `iso_rtm_hub_prices`. SPP: SPPNORTH_HUB real-time, 15-minute, `iso_hub_prices_history` (from 2024-09-01) and `iso_rtm_hub_prices`.
- **Method.** An hour is the local clock hour, its price the mean of its held intervals; it is negative when that mean is below the threshold (input: 0, -10 or -20 USD/MWh). Per hub and year: held hours, negative hours, their share, negative intervals and held intervals, the first and last day held. No cap, no exclusion. A year a hub does not cover is not held and not filled.
- **Chart.** Lines by year, one per hub, negative hours.

## Finding 7: "By how much do batteries cut curtailment" (econometric, session 174)

- **Question.** On days when CAISO's batteries charge more, is less curtailed, once solar output and the month are held fixed?
- **Data.** `caiso_curtailment_daily` (CAISO's production and curtailment report: curtailed solar and wind MWh a day), `caiso_battery_storage` (Today's Outlook, batteries MW every five minutes, from 2025-08-24; negative is charging), `caiso_fuel_supply` (Today's Outlook, solar MW by hour). Pacific days; a day needs at least 276 of its 288 battery intervals and 23 solar hours.
- **Method.** Charging MWh a day is the sum of the negative five-minute values times 5/60. Three OLS regressions of daily curtailment (MWh; input: solar and wind, or solar only) on charging (GWh): alone; with solar generation (GWh); with solar and month-of-sample dummies; HC1 standard errors (Stata's `vce(robust)`), p from Student's t. The effect table shows the three; the words state the third as an association. A plain comparison of means at the median charging day stands beside it. The subtitle follows the sign and the p value of the controlled estimate, whichever way it falls.
- **What it does not say.** A cause: the batteries' dispatch and the prices that call for it are not separated. The first computation (10 October 2026) found charging higher on the days with more curtailment, even with solar and month held fixed; the card says so.

## Analysis 8: "Impact of an event on a series" (econometric, session 181)

- **Question.** Around an event the warehouse holds, or any date, how did a series change, against a control series?
- **Inputs.** A series and a control (23 daily series by plain names: ERCOT hub prices, NYISO zone prices, SPP South Hub, CAISO ZP26, Henry Hub, WTI, Brent, and the daily demand of seven grids; `SERIES` in `warehouse/analysis/findings/impact_study.py` names each one's table); an event (the first day of each window in `event_window_daily`'s header: Uri, the COVID-19 spring, the 2020 and 2022 California heat waves, Elliott, the 2023 ERCOT heat, the January 2025 cold, the June 2025 heat) or a date (year, month, day); a window of 7, 14, 28 or 56 days before and after. Defaults: ERCOT North Hub real-time price, Winter Storm Uri, 14 days, NYISO New York City real-time price as the control. `/analysis` has the inputs with a Reset.
- **Data.** Daily values: a daily table's own days (ERCOT's local operating days), an hourly table's UTC days with at least 20 hours, as their mean.
- **Method.** Before and after are means over the W days before the date and the W days from it, on the days both series hold. The difference against the control is a difference in differences: the series' change minus the control's. The regression, on those days: (series minus control) = a + b * after + e, by least squares; b equals the difference in differences. Its standard error is Newey-West (heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent, Bartlett kernel) with L = floor(4 * (n / 100)^(2/9)) lags over the days in their order and the factor n / (n - 2), as Stata's `newey`; p from Student's t on n - 2 degrees of freedom. `statsmodels` is not in the warehouse's environment: `newey_west` in the module is the whole computation, and `tests/test_session181.py` proves it against a case worked by hand (y = 1, 3, 2, 6; one lag; standard errors the square roots of 0.5 and 3.5) and against the sums written out.
- **Parallel movement is shown, not asserted.** Reading b as the event's effect needs the two series to have moved in parallel without it. The card draws a second chart, the series minus the control over the 3 W days before the date, and its table gives that gap's slope per day with its own Newey-West error.
- **The sentence.** Written by code from the numbers: the means, the two changes, the difference and its standard error, and whether the difference is within 1.96 standard errors of zero. It never says what produced the difference.
- **Refusals.** The card says what is missing and draws nothing when: the control is the series itself or equals it on every day; the control is in another unit; the date does not exist; the date is outside what either series holds; fewer than 5 days are held by both series on either side.
- **Downloads.** CSV (one row per day from three windows before the date: `t`, `period`, `after`, `in_window`, `series_value`, `control_value`, `diff`), the Python, and a Stata do-file. The CSV begins with four comment lines that hold no comma, and the do-file reads past them: `import delimited "...", varnames(5) rowrange(6) stringcols(_all) clear`, then `tsset obs` and `newey diff after, lag(L)`.
- **Two cases (10 October 2026).** Uri (7 February 2021, 14 days): ERCOT North Hub real-time averaged USD 3,047.37 per MWh against USD 21.46 before; the control rose by USD 9.51; the difference is USD 3,016.40, standard error USD 1,598.93 (3 lags, 28 days), within 1.96 standard errors of zero because five days at the cap sit beside nine ordinary ones. A quiet date (15 May 2024, 14 days): the difference is minus USD 37.49, standard error USD 19.95: no difference beyond the error.
- **What it does not say.** A cause. Anything else that changed for one series and not the other inside the window is inside b. A control hit by the same event understates the difference.
- **Reading a requested card.** A card asked for on `/analysis` is written by the worker into its queue row and read from there (`analysis_request_card`, migration 029): the list's "show the card" and the impact form draw it with the same card component. Its downloads are made in the page from what the card carries: the CSV from the card's own rows under its four comment lines, the do-file from the card's text, the Python from the site's copy of the module. The default card's three files are committed under `site/public/findings/`.
- **Where it runs.** On the data machine, by the worker, from a request queued on `/analysis`. The series' tables are not restored on the Roundup's runner (`TABLES` names only `event_window_daily`).

### Finding 8: "Batteries ate their own lunch? Five grids" (econometric, `batteries_lunch_grids`)

- **Question.** Did real-time price spikes shrink as each grid's own battery fleet grew?
- **Prices, and where each panel starts.** ERCOT: HB_HUBAVG, 15-minute, from 2019-01 (`ercot_all_hub_prices_history`, `iso_rtm_hub_prices`). NYISO: the N.Y.C. zone, NYISO's own integrated hourly real-time price, from 2019-01 (`iso_zone_prices_history`). CAISO TH_SP15_GEN-APND, ISO-NE .H.INTERNAL_HUB and SPP SPPNORTH_HUB: 15-minute, from 2024-09-01, the first day the warehouse holds (`iso_hub_prices_history`, `iso_rtm_hub_prices`). A panel starts where its prices start; nothing earlier is filled. NYISO's measures are on hourly prices: an hour's mean hides a spike that quarter hours would show, so its level is not comparable with the 15-minute grids, and the card says so. ISO-NE's zone history from 2019 is an internal table and is not used.
- **Spike measures, per grid, local month and local year.** As finding 1: the median, the 99.9th percentile, their ratio (the worst-interval multiple), hours at or above USD 1,000 and USD 250 per MWh (intervals times their length), and hours at or above the 99th percentile of that grid's own sample (one fixed threshold per grid). A year is full when it holds at least 90 percent of its intervals (ISO-NE's 2025 holds 90.7 percent); a grid with one full year is compared with the year to date, labeled so.
- **The fleet.** `storage_buildout_monthly`, `battery_operating_mw` of each grid. A unit is a generator with prime mover BA in EIA-860M's inventory; it is assigned to a grid by its balancing authority code (ERCO, CISO, NYIS, ISNE, SWPP) and counted at nameplate MW from its first operating month until the month it retired ([`storage_buildout.md`](storage_buildout.md)). The inventory held is August 2026: later months carry no fleet figure and are left out of the regression.
- **Controls.** Load: ERCOT as finding 1 (`ercot_zone_load_hourly`); the other grids from `eia930_daily_demand` (the balancing authority's daily MWh over 24, the mean of the month's days, EIA's Eastern day). Gas: Henry Hub daily spot, monthly mean.
- **The regression, per grid.** Finding 1's: OLS of each monthly spike measure on battery GW, mean load in GW, Henry Hub and eleven month-of-year dummies (15 parameters), HC1 standard errors, p from Student's t. ERCOT and NYISO have 92 months; CAISO, ISO-NE and SPP have 24, which leaves 9 degrees of freedom: the card prints those three as descriptions, not tests. Where a fleet never reached 2 GW, "per GW" is an extrapolation and the caveat says so.
- **The chart.** Small multiples: one panel a grid on one month axis from 2019-01. The measure's axis is each grid's own; the fleet's axis (right, shaded) is one shared scale, so the fleets compare at a glance; both are stated on the chart. A control shows one grid large (the old card's chart, that grid's callouts, its three-row regression table, the effect in words and its caveat); the choice is kept in the address (`?lunch_grid=ercot`).
- **The old card.** The ERCOT panel is computed by finding 1's own functions; a test proves every number of the old card equals the new card's ERCOT number, row for row of the CSV.
- **What it does not say.** A cause. Nothing separates a fleet from the years it arrived in, and a grid with one year of prices has no before to compare. The subtitle is chosen by the data.

### Finding 9: "The peak hour moved: five grids" (visual, `peak_hour_grids`)

- **Question.** Has the hour of the day's highest real-time price moved as each grid's solar fleet grew?
- **Data and method.** Finding 4's, on five grids: the same prices as finding 8 (NYISO hourly, the others the mean of the hour's 15-minute prices), each grid's local time with daylight saving handled by the time zone conversion, a day's peak hour the clock hour with the highest mean (the first on a tie), a day needing 20 held hours. Per grid and year: the percent of days peaking in each hour, the median peak hour, the percent peaking 17:00 to 21:59 and 10:00 to 15:59. A year is full with at least 300 held days.

The one-grid view (`?peak_grid=nyiso`) is finding 4's chart over the 24 hours for that grid's first and last full years and the year to date.

- **What it does not say.** Why the hour moved: the card compares years and does not separate solar from gas prices, load growth, weather or batteries.

### Finding 10: "By how much do batteries cut curtailment, hour by hour" (econometric, `batteries_curtailment_hourly`)

- **Question.** Within a day, is less curtailed in the hours CAISO's batteries charge than net load alone would predict?
- **Data.** CAISO by the hour, Pacific time, 2019-01-01 on. Curtailment (solar plus wind, MW over the hour): `caiso_curtailment_intervals`, 5-minute MW to 2025-12-31 and hourly MWh from 2026; an hour not listed on a covered day is zero (CAISO lists only intervals with a curtailment). Charging and net load: `caiso_fuel_supply_history` and `caiso_fuel_supply`; charging is the batteries column below zero; demand is the sum of the thirteen sources, which is the load served other than charging, and net load is demand less solar and wind. CAISO's own load table begins in 2021-09, which is why demand is built from the fuel supply table.
- **Model.** OLS of hourly curtailment (MW) on charging (MW); on charging, net load (GW) and its square; on those with 23 hour-of-day and 93 month-of-sample dummies; and the last without charging, whose fitted value is "what net load alone predicts": its residual is averaged over the charging hours, by year. Standard errors are cluster-robust by Pacific day (Stata's `vce(cluster)` with its small-sample factor), because the hours of one day share its weather and prices. The card reports the Pearson correlation r of hourly curtailment with charging beside each R2.
- **The ceiling.** An hour is at the fleet's ceiling when charging is at or above 90 percent (input: 90 or 95) of (a) the largest charging hour of the 30 days before its day, or (b) the fleet's EIA-860M nameplate MW of its month (`storage_buildout_monthly`). By year: the hours at the ceiling, those with curtailment still on, the MWh curtailed in them and their share of the year's. The two measures differ and both are on the card: no hour of the sample reached 90 percent of nameplate.
- **What the coefficient is and is not.** The association of an hour's charging with that hour's curtailment, with net load, the hour and the month held fixed. It is not the curtailment batteries prevent: charging is chosen when prices are low, which is when solar is turned down. Net load as published is after curtailment; the footnote gives the estimate with curtailed MW added back.
- **The daily card (finding 7)** now reports the correlation coefficient r beside R2, in its numbers, paragraph, footnote, CSV header and do-file (`correlate`). Its other numbers are unchanged: it was rebuilt from its committed rows, not recomputed.

## The request flow and the chart forms (session 182, part 4, 10 October 2026)

On `/analysis` a request is made in two steps (`site/components/analysis/RequestFlow.tsx`). The flow takes the place of "Ask for a finding", of the impact study's own section and of the template gallery.

- **Step one, what to analyze.** One list (`site/lib/analysisflow.ts`), eighteen entries: seven findings, the impact study, ten weekly chart templates. The catalogue holds eleven analyses; three of the seven findings have two versions, so the seven entries cover ten catalogue ids: "Batteries ate their own lunch?" (five grids, current; one ERCOT hub, the earlier card), "The peak hour moved" (five grids, current; ERCOT and CAISO by hour, the earlier card), "By how much do batteries cut curtailment" (day by day; hour by hour). The version is a choice among the inputs. The two single-grid cards that a five-grid card supersedes are no longer in the list of findings; each is linked under its five-grid card and stays at its own address (`/analysis/card/batteries_lunch`, `/analysis/card/peak_hour_moved`).
- **The templates as analyses.** A template is computed by the weekly run (`warehouse/analysis/run.py`, unchanged) for every choice of its inputs and written to `docs/analysis/gallery/`. The flow shows that weekly result at once for the inputs chosen: it is not a request, nothing waits in the queue, and the page says so with the date of the last run. Nine templates are public and drawn: peak premium by time of day, day-ahead minus real-time by hour, demand forecast error, curtailment against midday prices, implied heat rate, batteries and the evening peak, negative-price hours by month, deals by month and type, datacenter facilities by state. A choice of inputs for which the weekly run found no data is kept in the list, marked "not held", with the run's reason. The tenth, tanker transits at the chokepoints, reads a table licensed internal: it is listed with its inputs and is not drawn on the site.
- **Step two, how to show it.** Five chart forms (`site/lib/chartforms.ts`): lines, paired bars, stacked bars, small multiples, points with a fitted line. Each form offered is drawn as a small example from real data: the analysis's committed card with its default inputs, or the template's chart of the week for the chosen inputs. The user flips through the forms with Previous and Next or presses a tile.
- **The fit rule: which forms are offered.** Only a form that can honestly show the data. Lines: the axis is ordered (months, years, days, hours of the day) and holds two positions or more; series in different units are drawn as lines only where the analysis's own drawing gives each unit its axis. Paired bars: every series is in one unit and the axis holds at most 31 positions. Stacked bars: the series are parts of one whole (shares that sum to 100 percent, counts that sum to a total). Small multiples: two to six series or grids, one panel each, each panel on its own scale. Points with a fitted line: pairs of two measures (one point a day); no other form keeps the pairing, so such an analysis has that form alone. A scanner draft, whose drawing marks a flagged point and reference lines, keeps its own form alone.
- **The default rule.** The preselected form is the one the analysis's own code declares, its chart kind: a timeline or the hours of a day as lines (`line_with_fleet`, `lines`, `flag_line`; a template whose series are lines), groups compared side by side as paired bars (`grouped_bar`, `bars_free`; a template whose series are bars), parts of a whole as stacked bars (`stacked_bar_pct`; a template whose bars are stacked), many grids as small multiples (`multiples`), two measures of each day as points (`scatter`). On the page it is labeled "Default for this analysis". The word is "default" because it is how the author of the analysis drew it; it is not advice, and the tool recommends nothing.
- **What each analysis is offered** (default first). Batteries ate their own lunch, five grids: small multiples, lines. One ERCOT hub: lines, small multiples. Gas sets the price: paired bars, lines, small multiples. Till queue do us part: stacked bars, paired bars, small multiples. The peak hour moved, five grids: small multiples, lines, paired bars. ERCOT and CAISO by hour: lines, paired bars, small multiples. Who rescues whom: paired bars, small multiples. Negative prices march west: lines, paired bars, small multiples. Curtailment day by day: points. Hour by hour: lines, paired bars, small multiples. Impact study: lines, paired bars, small multiples (the days after the event stay shaded in each). Templates: peak premium, paired bars and small multiples; day-ahead minus real-time, paired bars and lines; forecast error and implied heat rate, lines and paired bars; curtailment against midday prices, points; batteries and the evening peak, lines and small multiples (two units); negative-price hours, paired bars, and lines when two months or more are held; deals by month, stacked bars, lines, paired bars; datacenters by state, stacked bars, paired bars, small multiples.
- **What a form changes.** How the page draws the values, nothing else. No form computes a number: each reads the values the card's JSON or the template's weekly file holds, and hover shows them. In its default form a card is drawn exactly as before. In another form a card of many grids shows one measure at a time (a row of toggles picks it), and the hover of a redrawn chart names the series, its value and its unit, without the extra counts the default's hover carries.
- **What a request carries.** What it carried before: the finding and its inputs (`POST /api/analysis`, the queue of migration 027, unchanged; no migration was needed). The chart form is not part of the computation, so it travels in the address, not in the queue: `/analysis?analysis=<entry>&version=<catalogue id>&i.<input>=<value>&form=<form>&request=<request id>`. A link opens the same analysis, inputs, form and request, and the card that arrives is drawn in that form. A card already computed is re-shown in another form without a new request: in the flow ("Show the card computed ..."), on the card's own page (`/analysis/card/<id>?form=<form>`), and on any card of the list (the row "Shown as", kept in the address as `form.<card id>`).
- **Where it runs.** The flow is the site's own code. A finding's request needs the data machine, as before; when it is off the request stays queued and the page says so. A template's chart needs only the weekly files in the repository.
