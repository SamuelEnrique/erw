## How it was read, and what could not be

- **What the built site shows:** `datacenter_facilities` is held whole (428 rows, "license needed" from 2026-10-21), so the facilities view draws the ERCOT large-load section (public, `ercot_large_load_status`, 12 reports), the hold, the words, the power side (the two ERCOT queue sites, the load zones' prices) and the downloads line; the facilities list, "What changed this week", the rollups by grid and operator, the timeline and the three charts are not drawn at all. The by-grid view draws the queue sites, ERCOT's prices by year and the facilities-against-grid table with the hold's placeholder in every facility cell. The map draws 263 datacenters from the page's own copy of the same table.
- **So the rollups, the timeline, the grid loads and the charts were recomputed with the page's own functions** (`site/lib/dcrollups.ts`) in Node on the live set's 428 rows (`runs/s1854/recompute-dc.mjs`, `recompute.json`), and the rollups by hand in Python for ERCOT: 45 facilities read as ERCOT's (a named grid, else the state's main grid), 44 counted by the page's rule (a named US state, not withdrawn or cancelled; my first count kept one withdrawn row), 4 state MW (50, 222, 73 and 112), 457 MW summed, 40 counted beside the sum as stating none. The page's functions give the same (`rollups`: ERCOT 44, 457 MW, 4 with MW, 40 without).
- **The timeline's medians could not be recomputed for any grid:** `timeline()` finds 0 facilities with both dates in the live set (row 8 below says why), so there is no median to check.
- **At 390 px** no page scrolls sideways (390 of 390 on the three addresses). At 1280 the two tracker views draw at 1265 and the by-grid tables scroll inside their frame with the fade, as the brand says.
