# What a battery saves a customer, read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7513, 16 November 2026)

**The reader.** The energy manager of a grocery chain: stores of a few hundred kW each, on a commercial rate with a demand charge, an installer's quote for a battery in hand. Chosen of the four kinds of building named (a cold store, a supermarket, a school district, a small factory) because the page asks for one month and takes twelve alike, one discharge a day, and a peak inside the peak-rate hours: a store's refrigeration runs every day of every month and into the evening, so it is the building this arithmetic fits best; and the one commercial building with a published size, peak and battery in the documents part A read is a grocery store.
**The five cases.** One real store (27,625 square feet, a peak of about 247 kW in 2012: EnerNOC facility 384, a grocery store in Los Angeles, in NREL/TP-6A20-64987, November 2015, page 7) taken to five published rates of `replication/battery_customer/cases.json`, with a battery of a published size (55 kW and 110 kWh, the size that report's table 9 found best for the store; 100 kW and 400 kWh, a 4 hour commercial unit): (1) Seattle City Light, medium general service, time of use, City of Seattle, rates as read on 16 November 2026: what does each battery save in a year? (2) the same store on the Downtown Network's rate: does a demand charge of USD 12.19 against USD 5.36 change which battery? (3) Salt River Project E-32 (Arizona), November 2025 billing cycle: what is the year on a rate with three seasons? (4) Austin Energy, secondary voltage, 10 kW to under 300 kW, bills from 1 October 2026: does a battery pay where every hour has one price? (5) the store's own rate in NREL's work, Southern California Edison TOU-GS-2 Option B of about April 2013 as NREL/TP-5400-63162 prints it: can the page's figure be set beside the one published study of this store? The store's kind, size and peak and the rates are published; putting that store on another city's rate is the case, and is not data.
**How it was read, and what was not seen.** This machine cannot build the site. The page's own calculator (`site/app/battery/customer/Calc.tsx`) was drawn to HTML with each case's numbers in its nine fields, 19 draws in all, and every sentence and figure drawn was read; the decisions block was drawn and each of its 10 tips read; the page's words outside the calculator were read in `page.tsx`; the arithmetic for each variation is the page's own (`saving` in `site/lib/customerbattery.ts`). The other pages were read in their code (`site/lib/battery/finance.ts`, `site/lib/battery.ts`, `site/app/learn/bill/`, `site/lib/pages.ts`). No key was pressed, no hover was seen, nothing was seen at 390 px, and `site/scripts/check-no-request.mjs` was not run (it needs a served build and a browser). No web request was made: every rate and every fact of the store is from a file part A had already read. The tool reads no ERW table, so no table stands behind a figure.

## Two pages, one figure

- **Round trip.** Row 3: 86 here and on What a battery earns, 90 in the home battery game, one meaning. Held by the test against `site/lib/battery/finance.ts` and `site/lib/battery.ts`.
- **A battery's hours.** Here "(2.0 hours)" is energy over power, as the durations of What a battery earns (2, 4 and 8 hours) are. "How long your peak lasts" is another quantity under its own name. No disagreement.
- **Demand charge.** Here: USD per kW of the month's highest demand, typed. `/cost-of-power` reads a large load's filed rate in the same unit (USD per kW a month, `tariffParts` in `site/lib/siting.ts`) and spreads it over the load's MWh. One meaning. `/learn/bill` has none (row 7).
- **No shared table.** The tool reads none, so no figure of it can disagree with a warehouse table.

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **The hours their peak lasts.** No bill prints them; they are in the meter's 15 or 30 minute readings, which the page does not ask for. They move the answer more than any other field (case 5: USD 22,080 to USD 8,819), and the starting 2 hours has no source (part A, row 5).
- **A year on a rate with seasons.** The manager types each season in turn and adds the months by hand; the largest figure on the page is twelve of whichever was typed last (part A, row 3: USD 4,593 over on Salt River Project, USD 8,175 on the 2013 sheet).
- **What the battery takes off a real meter.** The demand saving is all of the battery's reach in every month; a load's shape decides the rest, and the page has no load. NREL's 85 percent is on hover, not in the figure (part A, row 6).
- **A figure to set against the quote.** The saving is a year's dollars on the bill. The quote is a price: no payback, no return, no saving for each kWh of battery is printed, and two quotes are typed in turn (part A, row 8). The one published study of this store gives a net present value with solar in it, not a bill saving.
- **The rest of the bill.** A second demand charge on the other hours (USD 1.02 at Salt River Project, USD 0.31 at Seattle City Light) that charging can raise, an allowance of uncharged kW (row 5), per kWh riders (Austin Energy's five), and the hour of the store's own peak against the rate's hours (row 6): each is a clause of a real rate the nine fields cannot hold.
