# The shoulder hours (`/shoulder`) read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7512, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a fund analyst (a storage investor's or a lender's) testing the claim that four hours of batteries is enough for the evening, in Texas and in California. Not a merchant developer choosing a node, and not a utility planner: every figure on the page is the whole grid's and the whole fleet's (hours needed fall as the fleet's MW grow), and the page holds no price, no node and no single battery.
**The five cases:** (1) is four hours enough for ERCOT's evening in August 2025, and was it in August 2023; (2) how much of California's evening did the fleet cover in April 2026 against April 2024; (3) the worst day of ERCOT's 2025, how long and how deep; (4) what the Texas page says in the months net load was lowest before dawn (August 2023; May 2024, the last of them); (5) California in August 2025 by CAISO's own data against EIA-930's.
**What the reader wants next, and does not find here:** the price of those hours; the page sends them to `/cost-of-power/battery` (rows 8 and 9).

**How it was read.** This machine cannot build the site. The page was drawn to HTML with the site's own code (`site/scripts/shoulder-figures.mjs`, part A's script) on the table as this machine holds it (`shoulder_hours_monthly`, built 4 October 2026, 27,201 rows, months to September 2026): 193 views, each grid and month, and the cases' addresses one by one (a month the grid does not hold, a grid the page does not hold). Every cell, every hover line of the two charts and every tip of the cases' views was read as text. The neighbouring pages were read through their own tables and files (`storage_buildout_monthly`, `site/data/curtailment/shares.json`, `site/data/demand_growth.json`, `battery_stack_monthly`), not drawn. No click was made, no chart was seen, no tip was seen open: what only a browser shows is in the session's report.
