Events / Winter Storm Elliott
Winter Storm Elliott: six grids, December 2022
FERC and NERC write that the storm reached the central U.S. by December 21, 2022, "eventually blanketing most of the eastern United States on December 23 and 24, and did not subside until December 26". At its worst "there were 90,500 MW of coincident unplanned generating unit outages, derates and failures to start" ( FERC, NERC and the Regional Entities, inquiry report ). PJM writes that "at one point, almost a quarter of the generation capacity", 47,000 MW, was on forced outages, and that its load on Dec. 23 "came in at about 136,000 MW" against a forecast of about 127,000 MW ( PJM, Winter Storm Elliott Event Analysis and Recommendation Report ).
This page sets the demand served by six grids, their peak hour each day and the carbon intensity of their generation over 2022-12-19 to 2022-12-29 against the same weekdays 364 and 728 days earlier, with ERCOT's hub prices. PJM's prices are not public in the warehouse.
Demand here is demand served. PJM writes that on Dec. 23 and Dec. 24 it "remained reliable, was able to serve its customers"; the balancing authorities that shed firm load were in the southeast U.S. (FERC and NERC), among them TVA and Duke, which PJM writes "were both in an EEA-3 and shedding load". The baseline weeks hold Christmas too (the same weekdays of 2021 and 2020). Method .

Decisions on this page (13): each choice that moves a figure, with its default and its source
Each event is a fixed window of local days, both ends included. Every "highest" and "lowest" of the page is taken inside it; a longer or shorter window could move the day and the figure picked, and moves every estimate. i
[tip] Default: 2022-12-19 to 2022-12-29. Source: docs/methods/events.md, each event's section; the dates the pages cite are the operators' and the government's accounts named in the framing. Made in: sessions 36B to 308.
An event day is set against the same days of earlier years: the same weekday 364 and 728 days earlier, so a Monday meets a Monday. A calendar-day baseline would set a Sunday against a Monday. i
[tip] Default: The same weekday 364 and 728 days earlier, both years held. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Baseline: weekday-aligned" and "Session 49". Made in: sessions 36B, 36C and 49.
A day is the grid's own local day: Central for ERCOT and SPP, Eastern for PJM, NYISO, ISO-NE and the Lower 48, Pacific for CAISO, and Eastern Standard Time all year for MISO, its market day. A day in UTC would move an evening peak to the next day. i
[tip] Default: Each grid's own zone. SPP's highest hour of Winter Storm Elliott, 18:00 Central on 2022-12-22, is 00:00 UTC on 2022-12-23. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "The second event: COVID-19", Days. Made in: session 36C.
A day's value is written only when every interval of it is held: 24 EIA-930 hours, 96 real-time intervals, 24 day-ahead hours (23 and 92 on the spring change). A day with one hour missing has no point and no comparison; nothing is filled. i
[tip] Default: Complete days only. CAISO's 2020-08-10, MISO's 2021-12-27 and ISO-NE's 2025-06-25 are among the days left out. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Complete days only" and each event's "Left out". Made in: sessions 36B and 36C.
Demand is demand served (EIA-930's hourly demand), not what customers would have used. During rotating outages the load that was shed is not in it, so a figure of those days is a floor. i
[tip] Default: EIA-930 demand served, from EIA's per-balancing-authority workbooks (the 2018 baseline from EIA's six-month file). Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Demand during load shed is load served". Made in: session 36B.
The peak hour against the baseline is the day's highest hour over the mean of the baseline days' highest hours, minus one, in percent: each day's own peak, whatever hour it fell in. i
[tip] Default: demand_max_pct_vs_baseline; a day needs the event day and at least one baseline day complete. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Session 39", the variables max_vs. Made in: session 39.
A figure under a chart is a row of the table picked by the rule its sentence states (the highest, the lowest, or a named day); of two equal values the earlier day is taken. The page computes no value of its own outside the estimates. i
[tip] Default: The first of equal values in day order. Source: site/app/events/EventWindow.tsx; replication/events/replicate.py recomputes each pick from the rule (docs/methods/events.md, "The figures on the pages"). Made in: session 39.
Carbon intensity is the day's CO2 from generation (EIA's estimates) times 1,000 over its net generation, in kg CO2 per MWh generated: not per MWh consumed, and imports carry none. i
[tip] Default: carbon_intensity_daily's formula, over the grid's local day. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Sources and variables"; eia930_all_emissions. Made in: session 36B.
Price is ERCOT's hub average (HB_HUBAVG): the day's mean and highest 15-minute real-time price and the day-ahead mean and highest hour. Since the January 2025 cold, four other hubs from 2024-09-01, with no baseline days; MISO's real-time price is hourly. i
[tip] Default: ERCOT HB_HUBAVG; MISO Indiana Hub, NYISO N.Y.C., ISO-NE Internal Hub, SPP North Hub. PJM's prices are internal and not used. Source: docs/methods/events.md, "Sources and variables" and "Session 64"; ERCOT NP6-785-ER and NP4-180-ER; iso_hub_prices_history. Made in: sessions 36B and 64.
The estimate is a regression of each day's value on the event days, six weekday indicators (Monday the reference) and the baseline years coded to sum to zero; the event year takes none, so its counterfactual is the average baseline year. Growth since the baseline years counts as effect. i
[tip] Default: dow_year_mean_v1. A linear trend in place of the year effects (dow_trend_v1) is in the table, not on the page. Source: docs/methods/event_study.md, "Specification" and "Identification and what it assumes". Made in: session 47.
Intervals are 95 percent, normal: estimate plus or minus 1.96 robust (HC1) standard errors; a single day's error adds the baseline fit's residual variance. A t interval would be somewhat wider on baselines of 22 to 92 days. i
[tip] Default: HC1, z = 1.96. Source: docs/methods/event_study.md, "Inference". Made in: session 47.
The second estimate adds the day's heating and cooling degree days at 65 F and their squares, the mean over the grid's airports, the day's mean taken as (max + min) / 2; days without weather are left out of it. Events with no station held have no second estimate. i
[tip] Default: ERCOT DFW and IAH, CAISO SAC and LAX, PJM PHL and ORD, MISO MSP, NYISO JFK, ISO-NE BOS, SPP OKC; none for the Lower 48, the January 2025 cold, the June 2025 heat and the January 2026 cold. Source: docs/methods/event_study.md, "Temperature-controlled specification"; NOAA NCEI ISD (noaa_isd_hourly). Made in: session 49.
The share the weather terms account for, 1 minus the controlled estimate over the first, is printed only when the two share a sign and the controlled one is no larger in size; otherwise the page says no share can be put down to weather. i
[tip] Default: Printed only under that condition. Source: docs/methods/event_study.md, "What the pages say". Made in: session 49.
