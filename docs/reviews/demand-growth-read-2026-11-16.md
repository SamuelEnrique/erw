# The demand growth explorer (`/explore/demand-growth`) read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7517, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** an AI lab's energy lead sizing a 300 MW site that runs every hour, choosing among ERCOT, PJM and California. The page answers the first question of that shortlist (how fast, and in which hours, each grid's demand has grown since 2019), and that reader tests it hardest: they need the growth in MW, the newest peak and the forecast next. A fund analyst testing "load growth is back" brings cases 1, 2 and 4 as they stand; a utility planner would bring a zone, and the page holds none.
**The five cases:** (1) how much has ERCOT's demand grown since 2019, and how much of it is in the evening hours; (2) is PJM growing: the year so far of 2026 against 2019, and its summer peak; (3) a 300 MW site that runs every hour: what share of the growth since 2019 is that in ERCOT, in PJM and in the Lower 48; (4) California: plus 4.45 percent here for 2019 to 2025 where EIA's retail sales fell 2.96 percent; (5) MISO and SPP: what the page shows for a grid whose own publisher the ERW has paused.
**What the reader wants next, and did not find here:** a forecast. The page linked `/demand`, which holds none; since row 9 it says where the ERW holds the operators' own.

**How it was read.** This machine cannot build the site. The page was drawn to HTML with the site's own code (`site/scripts/demand-growth-figures.mjs`, part A's script) on the table as this machine holds it (`eia930_demand_growth`, built 6 November 2026, 24,116 rows, the year so far to October 2026): 315 views, every grid in every window, the page as it opens and an address the table does not hold. Every headline, every cell of the two tables and every hover line of the three charts of the cases' views was read as text, and the decisions' tips. The neighbouring pages were read through their own files and code (`site/data/demand_growth.json`, `site/data/siting/headroom.json`, `site/lib/siting.ts`), not drawn. California's charging was computed from `shoulder_hours_monthly` and `eia_retail_sales_monthly` (read only). No click was made, no chart was seen, no tip was seen open, nothing was asked of any publisher: what only a browser shows is row 12 and the session's report. A gap part A wrote down (`docs/reviews/demand-growth-faults-2026-11-16.md`, 19 rows) is cited by its row and not written again.

## The neighbouring pages, on the same cases

- **`/demand` (the lab desktop's tool; not edited).** It reads `site/data/demand_growth.json`: all 24,116 values of that file equal the table's rows as held today, so no figure both pages draw can differ (ERCOT +27.22, PJM's year so far +8.34, CAISO +4.45, the peaks). It already draws the newest year's highest hour (PJM 162,648 MW), which this page lacked until row 3. No row.
- **`/cost-of-power?view=allgrids` (Headroom).** Its peak of the last whole year is this table's for all seven grids (PJM 160,560 MW, ERCOT 83,597, CAISO 43,860, MISO 120,343, SPP 54,411, NYISO 31,857, ISO-NE 25,898). Its demand growth is another period from the same rows (row 10). Its forecasts are the only ones on the site (rows 9 and 11): ERCOT's reserves report, CAISO's summer assessment, and the revision of the 2030 summer peak between the 2024 and 2025 filings of FERC Form 714 (PJM -875 MW, MISO +5,485, SPP +7,235, CAISO -2,442, ERCOT +0.4: chain 302's row 5). It draws no forecast growth rate to set against this page's history: for PJM a reader has +5.41 percent in six years of peaks here and a revision of -0.48 percent there, and no forecast level.
- **`/cost-of-power` ("Your load").** Its model reads the last whole year's rows of the same file (`site/app/cost-of-power/model.ts`, line 303), which equal this table's; it computes no growth of its own. Not drawn here. No row.
- **`/datacenters`.** It draws no figure of this table. No row.
- **`/shoulder` (session 7512, on `wip/7503-shoulder`).** Its California demand is the same EIA-930 series to November 2025, and its second source, CAISO's own, is the one row 5 computes the charging from. Its rows 5, 7 and 13 and this file's rows 5, 6 and 8 are one finding seen from two tools.

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **The growth in MW for the year, and a site's share of it.** The table writes a change in MW for a month and an hour only (row 1), and the page takes no input (row 2): the 300 MW case is the reader's own arithmetic on two cells.
- **A forecast.** The table holds none. The site's forecasts are three kinds of figure on one tab (row 9), with no forecast growth to set beside the history here, and a forecast and a metered hour are not one measure (row 11).
- **A place smaller than a grid.** Every figure is a whole balancing authority's. ERCOT West, Dominion's zone or a utility's own area are not here; chain 302's read of the All grids tab wrote the same gap (its row 8).
- **California's growth without the batteries.** The charging is in EIA's demand; it is held by month from June 2025 only, so the year 2025 cannot be split (rows 5, 6 and 8), and March 2019 may be a low base (row 7).
- **The newest year by hour or by month as growth.** The evening of 2026 is not a figure: the table writes an hour's and a month's growth on the last whole year only (part A's row 14), and the hours take one address each (part A's row 18).
