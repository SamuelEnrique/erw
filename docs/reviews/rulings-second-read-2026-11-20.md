# The rulings read a second time (20 November 2026)

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW). Written by session 9801 on Samuel's own laptop, at his instruction of 20 November: a second reader of the 54 recommended switches and 48 recommended keeps of [the rulings for one sitting](https://github.com/SamuelEnrique/erw/blob/main/docs/reviews/rulings-one-sitting-2026-11-19.md), whose evidence one session (chain 503) gathered on 19 November. For each entry the evidence it cites was opened again: a publisher's page only after its robots file and terms; one of Samuel's rulings in the ruling itself under `docs/state/`; the tool's method note under `docs/methods/`. Two things were judged: does the evidence say what the entry claims, and does it decide this question. Nothing was changed: no recommendation in the ranked list or the sheet, no ruling taken, nothing landed.

- **How it was read:** six readers worked at once, each given the entries and the method above and none of the first session's conclusions beyond what the entry itself says; each row was written by the reader that opened the evidence. Entries are in the ranked list's order, which is the largest change first inside each of its sections.
- **Verdicts:** holds; holds weakly (the evidence is right but decides the question only partly, or by a like case); does not hold (the evidence does not say it, or does not decide this question); could not check (the reason is given).

## Do not rule these by group

Each entry below is not "holds": rule it on its own, after its row. The group line in the template of the weekend pack should name it as an exception.

- **4** (S1), could not check: The figure the recommendation rests on (Berkeley Lab counts 17 Texas projects of entry year 2022 operating) is from Berkeley Lab's queue data, whose host emp.lbl.gov answered 403; the repository only repeats it. If true it supports the switch, though it is counted at the end of 2025, a slightly different date and definition from approval to synchronize.
- **6** (S4), holds weakly: EIA's page does call the low of net load the midday dip, when solar output is highest, and the method note (shoulder.md section 10, item 4) admits the Texas figure is taken in the night and leaves the rule to the owner. But EIA describes the duck curve, not a measuring rule; it argues as well for renaming the figure as for re-ruling it, and the 5 percent daylight threshold has no source.
- **8** (S2), holds weakly: The ruling exists (roadmap: 12 October 2026, New York shows day-ahead first, answering G3 on 19 missing days) and /queues writes no share under 20 requests. But the ruling is about a series with missing days, a like case stretched to an opening year, and the floor of 20 is a rule for writing a share, not for choosing which year a page opens on.
- **51** (S4), holds weakly: The state tax office page does list the Oil and Gas Ad Valorem Production Tax among the five taxes on the value of gas, so leaving it out understates the state's own tally. The "statewide average 0.88 percent" claim rests on legislature PDFs I could not read as text (compressed PDF, no extractor here), so the rate to add is unverified.
- **2** (K2), holds weakly: The method note does say demand is shown at its own newest hour and the exchange's two-day lag is printed, and EIA confirms demand comes hourly while generation and interchange come a day or more late. But the note describes what the page does rather than weighing the common-hour option, and the 18 October ruling (3c) is about stating the age of the network's hour, a like case, not the three figures of the Mix sentence.
- **5** (K3), holds weakly: Section 18 of the method note does say a grid operator's action that names no state is under Grid, as the hover tells the reader. That line describes the page as built (written by a read in session 7204), not a decision that weighed listing PJM-wide actions under each state, so it is the current rule rather than a ruling; the point that five PJM places are only partly in PJM is a fair reason on its own.
- **11** (K2), does not hold: The entry says the method note records this as already ruled, but "(a ruling)" in the note marks a ruling still waiting: the note of 13 November lists it under "Rulings waiting (default in brackets)", item 7, default 0. Nothing in docs/state shows Samuel ruled it; the remaining point (the Comptroller's letter rulings reach generating equipment, not every item, so the share is unknown) is a reason to weigh, not a decision, and the letter ruling itself was not opened here.
- **15** (K2), holds weakly: Rule 9 of chain 302 (Samuel's standing page rules) does say "an upgrade drops nothing the page had" and "two cases side by side wherever a reader would compare", and the branch drops the 84-figure side-by-side table. But these are general rules, and the method note itself says which demand the measure should stand on is still the owner's, so the standard argues against dropping the table rather than settling which source the headline uses.
- **37** (K1), holds weakly: Epoch AI does keep roles in separate fields and shows "Unknown", but its fields are chip owner and user, not operator and developer, and it says nothing about filling one from another; EIA's own rule presumes the owner is the operator when no owner file row exists, which is filling a role, not leaving it unknown. The session 16 ruling is about the extractor not inferring state, country or status from story words, a like case stretched.
- **75** (K2), holds weakly: Ruling 4 of 6 November says a row whose only source asks written permission stays internal, and the license file does record all nine operators as needing permission (three of them by default, never read), so the 210 rows fail that test. But ruling 4 was made for the large-load tariff table, not this one, so it is a like case applied, not a ruling on this table.
- **79** (K3), holds weakly: The page standard does say every figure carries its source document and real numbers only, and the method note says geothermal has no taxable share unless the reader types one. But the standard is a general rule, not a ruling on this choice, and I did not read the two Nevada notes behind the 3.3 and 48 percent figures.
- **80** (K3), holds weakly: The method note does say no price is filled in, converted or estimated, but that is a session's own default (session 83), not an owner ruling, and it describes the page as built rather than deciding whether a second, labelled figure may stand beside the filed one. I did not open the FERC data dictionary for lack of time.
- **81** (K2), holds weakly: Ruling 6a of 19 October says a figure naming NYISO or ISO-NE reads "license needed" with the card staying, which decides the New England card. It names only those two grids, so for the two cards built from the Texas commission's internal table it is a like case stretched, not a ruling.
- **84** (K2), holds weakly: Rule 5 of the owner's chain file of 16 November does say a license hold is never lifted or worked around, and rule 9 says a held value is drawn blank with "license needed". But that rule bars a session from lifting it, it does not choose between waiting for permission and taking the commission's rows out, and I did not find the Texas commission's hold named in the license code by a quick search.
