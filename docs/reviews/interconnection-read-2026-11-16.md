# The interconnection queue explorer read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7518, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a renewable or storage developer deciding which grid operator's queue to file a project in. Chosen over the AI lab's energy lead because every figure on the page is a generator's request (entered, withdrawn, reached operation, the wait from entry to operation): the developer's own questions. What a large load's reader can and cannot take is the section before the last.
**Five cases:** (1) SPP, entry year 2018: of what entered, how much reached operation and how long did it wait; (2) CAISO, entry year 2021, the year of its cluster 14 window: how much withdrew; (3) ERCOT, entry year 2020: what share of what entered is operating, given that ERCOT's report keeps only positions still in it; (4) a storage developer comparing the median wait of CAISO, ERCOT and SPP for the last entry year with at least ten finished projects; (5) the reader who wants PJM, MISO, NYISO or ISO-NE: what the page says.
**How it was read:** Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), page `/explore/interconnection`, in review. This machine cannot build the site. The page was drawn to HTML by its own code on `iso_queue_statistics` (`site/scripts/interconnection-figures.mjs --only=<address>`, 16 addresses, then all 284 views), every cell and hover read as text; the figures of the other pages were read from their own files on this machine (`site/data/queues.json`, `site/data/siting/connect.json`) and from the queue tables (`replication/interconnection/rows/`, the pull of 30 September 2026). No click was made and no chart was seen: an axis is known here only by the tick words in the drawn HTML. The table is the build of 21 October 2026 from the pulls of 19 October (part A's rows 9 and 10, not asked again): every figure of every case below is the queue as it stood on 19 October, and the queue tables on this machine are an older pull still, so a count taken from them (rows 1 and 6) is of 30 September.

Part A's file is `docs/reviews/interconnection-faults-2026-11-16.md` (18 rows); a gap it holds is cited as "A" and its row, not written again.

## What a large load's reader can and cannot take from this page

- **Can take:** how many plants and batteries asked to connect in a grid in a year and how many of them withdrew (CAISO and SPP), how many signed an agreement (ERCOT), and how long the built ones waited: the supply side of a site's grid, by entry year.
- **Cannot take:** anything about a load. No position on the page is a customer; no figure is a 300 MW site's wait, its study, its cost or its place in line. The page now says so and names the two places that hold the load side. `/queues` holds no load either (it is Queued Up, generators); ERCOT's large-load status (MW approved to energize and observed running, by report) is on `/datacenters`; the "Time to connect" column of `/cost-of-power?view=allgrids` sets a grid's generator wait beside the large-load rules held, ERCOT's approved MW, and the measured large-load waits (held under a license).

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **ERCOT's plants in operation by entry year, and so any completion share for ERCOT.** The table's group is a signed agreement (rows 1 and 2), ERCOT's report drops withdrawn positions and most finished plants (A, row 3), and the page divides nothing. Queued Up has it on `/queues`, in a bar's hover (row 12).
- **Storage, or any technology, on this page.** The queue tables hold it; the statistics table does not carry it (row 6).
- **A wait that is final for a recent entry year.** Only built positions have a wait; the newest years with ten of them are 2018 (CAISO), 2022 (ERCOT) and 2017 (SPP), and SPP's date may be the one requested. By the year online the figure is on two other pages (row 4).
- **PJM, and the operators' own figures for MISO, NYISO and ISO-NE.** The ERW holds no PJM queue table; the three are held by a license (row 9). Queued Up's counts of all four are one link away (rows 7 and 8).
- **Today's queue, a cluster or a study stage, a position's cost or its reason for leaving.** The table is one pull, of 19 October 2026, built 21 October (A, rows 9 and 10); it holds entry years, not CAISO's clusters or SPP's study groups; no queue report read here states a cost or a reason.
