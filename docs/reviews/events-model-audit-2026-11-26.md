# The events pages (`/events`): their figures audited as a hostile expert would

Session 10225, chain 710 part D, 26 November 2026, the old laptop. Read as a grid reliability analyst and an economist who write about these events and want to find the pages wrong. Platform tool: the Historical Event Analyzer, `/events` and its nine event pages. Three views taken whole: **Winter Storm Uri** (`/events/uri-2021`, the index's first card, so its default event), **the August 2020 heat wave in CAISO** (`/events/caiso-heat-2020`) and **Winter Storm Elliott** (`/events/elliott-2022`), with the index's three cards for them. Earlier reads are not repeated: the faults file of 16 November (`docs/reviews/events-faults-2026-11-16.md`: the hold that draws every EIA-930 figure "license needed", the COVID-19 baseline words, ERCOT's September record), the cross-check (`events-crosscheck-2026-11-16.md`), the ruling alternatives of 17 November (`ruling-alternatives-events-2026-11-17.md`: the temperature column for Elliott's days, Uri's offer cap count, FERC's SPP peak) and the regions read of 19 November. Window F's `/events/covid-2020` phone fix is not redone.

- Nothing changed on main but this file and its evidence folder (`docs/reviews/events-model-audit-2026-11-26/`). No table written, no lock taken, no default moved, no model called, no live service asked.
- Each finding has its own branch, `wip/audit-events-<n>`. The words-only fixes are for landing; the figure fixes carry a test only and wait for the owner.
- **Out of bounds: the paper's.** "The whole country" section's net imports columns, the hover that sets a region's pairs against its net imports, and the table "Power between regions, pair by pair" (who sent and who took) are the ground of the donor grids paper, Samuel's own work by hand: not restated, not audited, not touched.

## In short

- **The arithmetic is right.** Every figure the three views draw came out to the digit in an independent restatement from the page's own rows and files (numpy's least squares and its own HC1 for the regressions, not the site's code): Uri 246 of 246, CAISO 2020 213 of 213, Elliott 275 of 275, the index's three cards and their regions line 9 of 9. Out of bounds and not compared: the net imports and the pair table (the paper's).
- **The weather share is a product of the fit, not a measurement (findings 1 and 2, major).** "The weather terms account for 80.56 percent" (Uri), "65.33" (Elliott, PJM), "70.47" (CAISO 2020) come from a pooled regression that fits the weather terms on the event days too; each day's estimate on the same block fits them on the baseline days alone. Under that rule Uri's temperature-controlled effect is -92,180 MWh a day, not +30,284, and neither Uri nor PJM has any share. Half of Uri's days and 6 of PJM's 11 were colder than any baseline day.
- **"Not on the outage days" measures the wrong hour (findings 3 and 4, major).** The bold sentence on CAISO 2020 sets each day's highest hour of demand served against its baseline; the Stage 3 outages came at 18:36 and 18:27, as solar fell, and the shed load is not in demand served. By California's demand less solar and wind, 14 August was the highest day of 14 to 19 August (54,390 MW against 52,013 on the 18th); CAISO's gross peaks were 619 MW apart.
- **CAISO 2020's carbon intensity has no hydro under it (findings 5 and 6, moderate).** EIA's file holds no CAISO hydro from October 2019 to 24 August 2020: the window's intensity averages 281 kg/MWh against 185 and 205 on the baseline days. The ERW's own rule would leave all 14 days out; it is held, and the page said nothing.
- **The windows' edges are the ERW's, and three were chosen by looking (finding 7, moderate).** No account names an edge. Cut to the days its own account names, the pooled effect rises by 29 to 73 percent (Uri +155,795 to +269,649 MWh a day). Three windows were picked as the days of the highest hours, so their estimates lean high and their intervals do not allow for it.
- **Uri's price sat at the ceiling (finding 8, moderate).** On 16 to 18 February the hub's daily mean stood within 0.6 percent of the day's highest interval, about USD 9,000: the price figures and the estimate (+2,293.84 USD/MWh, "9,850.47 percent of the expected") measure the cap as much as scarcity.
- **Uri's "largest shortfall ... after the storm" is mostly a Sunday set against weekdays (finding 9, minor).** -186,530.50 MWh by the calendar day; -37,630.50 by the weekday rule every later event uses.
- **Words branches 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 for landing** (merged and the pin rewritten in `wip/audit-events-words`); figures 2, 4, 6, 9 wait for the owner, default left as it is. Held grids reach no drawn number on these pages; Elliott's words "ISO-NE barely moved" carry a held figure's sense (the hold's ruling, not a new finding).

## Each claim against the code

- **The window, its start and end, who chose them.** Each event's window is a constant in `event_window.py` and `lib/eventstudy.ts` `WINDOWS`, set by the session that added it. The decisions block said a window "moves every estimate" and that "the dates the pages cite are the operators' and the government's accounts"; it did not say the edges were the ERW's, and the defaults of CAISO 2020 and Elliott gave dates with no reason. The accounts the framing quotes name narrower days (the Root Cause Analysis: 14 and 15 August, the shortfalls projected for 17 to 19; FERC and NERC: 21 to 26 December). Three windows (January 2025, June 2025, January 2026) were chosen from the data as the days of the highest hours, said in their defaults, with no word of what that does to an estimate. **Finding 7.**
- **The baseline, and whether it was chosen after looking.** A fixed rule: the same weekday 364 and 728 days earlier for every event after Uri, and for Uri the same calendar days of 2019 and 2020, set in session 36B before the weekday rule. Not chosen after looking at results. Uri's calendar baseline sets weekdays against weekends, said in the decisions block ("A calendar-day baseline would set a Sunday against a Monday") but not under the figure it moves most. **Finding 9.** The event study absorbs weekdays with its own indicators, so its estimates are not touched by this.
- **Demand against load shed.** Every page says demand is demand served, and that the load shed is not in it (Uri's caveat, CAISO 2020's caveat, Elliott's caveat quoting PJM and FERC). True of the code: `demand_mwh` is EIA-930's demand, nothing added back. The event study's sentence says "daily demand served". Two places rest on it without saying so: the weather share fitted through Uri's days of load shed (finding 1), and CAISO 2020's comparison of the outage days (finding 3). The size of the shed load is not held (data request a).
- **Prices at the offer cap.** Uri's "highest 15-minute real-time price" (9,051.55 USD/MWh) is the hub average at the cap, and the daily mean sat there for three days; the page's caveat and the event study said nothing (**finding 8**; the count of intervals near the cap is the ruling alternative `wip/alt-events-4`, not repeated). Elliott's ERCOT hub reached 3,701.72 on 23 December, below the cap of that year (5,000 USD/MWh, `ercot_peak_premium.md`'s source); nothing to say.
- **EIA-930 revisions and impossible hours.** The table rests on EIA's per-BA workbooks as downloaded on 13 and 14 October 2026 (the table's header); the region section on `eia930_region_hourly` built 21 November, another vintage, and the lead's hover says a region is not a grid. The impossible-hours rule is written into the builder and held; for these three events it moves no row but CAISO 2020's 14 days of carbon intensity, through the hydro gap (**findings 5 and 6**; `impossible_hours.md`'s trial: no row of Texas moves, the other rows it takes are of April and May). Uri's hours are used whole (`test_session118`).
- **Time zones and local days.** Each grid's own local day, MISO on Eastern Standard Time all year, said in the decisions block and under each chart; Elliott's SPP hour of 18:00 Central on 22 December (00:00 UTC on the 23rd) is explained on the page. No window crosses a change of clock. The region section is in UTC hours "from the first day's midnight, Eastern time, to the midnight after the last day, Pacific time" (435 hours for Uri's 18 days), said in its hover. Right.
- **Held grids.** On these three pages no ISO-NE, MISO or NYISO hub is drawn. Their EIA-930 rows are drawn "license needed" with every other EIA-930 figure (fault 1 of 16 November, a ruling). The region section draws New England, the Midwest and New York from EIA's region data (public domain; `REGIONS_HELD` empty, a ruling of session 8410). Elliott's sentence "ISO-NE barely moved: its peak hour was at most [license needed] percent above the baseline" carries the held figure's sense in words; it is the hold's question and is left to that ruling.
- **A comparison worded as a cause.** "The weather terms account for N percent" reads as a cause and is a property of one fit (**findings 1 and 2**). "Not on the outage days" invites the reading that demand was not what ran the grid short (**finding 3**). Uri's "after the storm" on a Sunday's shortfall (**finding 9**). "The forced outages of Dec. 24 do not show in demand served: PJM served its load" rests on PJM's own report, quoted: right. "During the outages net generation stayed above that average" is true of the rows (+189,075 to +265,624 MWh, the replication's statement check).
- **The event study's other words.** "Robust standard errors", "95 percent", "Neither controls for anything else that happened in the same days": true of the code (HC1, z = 1.96). The percent of the expected for a price (Uri's hub, 9,850.47 percent) is arithmetic on a counterfactual near USD 23 and says nothing a reader can use; left as it is, not a finding.

### 1 and 2. Where the weather terms are fitted

- **Where.** `lib/eventstudy.ts` `estimate`: the day effects fit `design(..., pooled=false, Z)` on the baseline rows alone; the pooled effect fits `design(..., pooled=true, Z)` on every row, event days included. `event_study.py` the same. `EventStudy.tsx` prints `1 - t.pooled / s.pooled` as "the weather terms account for".
- **Why it matters.** With one window indicator, the event days still carry the four weather terms: their day-to-day swing in demand against degree days bends the curve. On Uri that swing includes 15 to 18 February, when demand served fell because load was shed while the cold deepened, so the curve learns that the coldest days need less power, and the share it leaves to "the event" shrinks. Fitted on the baseline days alone, the curve is carried beyond them: Uri's heating degree days reached 50.0 against a baseline highest of 30.0.
- **How much.** `measures.json`, "weather_rule": Uri ERCOT demand 80.56 percent (the page) against no share (the day rule gives -92,180 MWh a day against +143,554 without weather); the hub price 87.45 against 0.63; CAISO 2020 70.47 against 63.86; Elliott PJM 65.33 against no share (+742,712 against +387,000); MISO 46.92 against 1.16; ERCOT 73.11 against 25.25; SPP 67.97 against 42.90; ISO-NE 62.97 against 25.58; NYISO 76.13 against 63.87.
- **Finding 1, the fix on the branch.** Words only, after the share sentence on every page with a weather estimate: "The weather terms here are fitted on the event days and the baseline days together, while each event day's estimate below fits them on the baseline days alone, so the two can differ widely; and where an event day was colder or hotter than any baseline day, the curve is carried beyond the days it was fitted on. So a share, where one is given, is a property of this one fit, not a measured part of the effect that the weather caused." `event_study.md`: the pooled bullet corrected, and a section "Where the weather terms are fitted" with the table above. Test `tests/test_audit_events_1.py` (the words fail on main; the two rules recomputed from the replication's public rows pass). Drawn and read: pass.
- **Finding 2, for the owner.** Printing no share where an event day lies outside the baseline's degree days, or fitting the weather terms on the baseline days alone, moves the printed share: Uri 80.56 to none, CAISO 70.47 to 63.86 (or none: 8 of its days are hotter than any baseline day), PJM 65.33 to none.
