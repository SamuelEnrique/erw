# The Flex Alert scorecard (`/grid/caiso/alerts`) read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7546, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a demand-response analyst at CAISO or a California utility, or a fund analyst valuing demand response, who asks how much load an alert evening really took off, and how sure the estimate is.
**The five cases:** (1) 6 September 2022, the evening of the Energy Emergency Alert 3 and the state's text alert: the MW taken off in the alert hours, 16:00 to 21:00, and its interval; (2) 17 August 2020, a Stage 2 evening with a Flex Alert from 15:00 to 22:00: the same, set against what CAISO itself published for that day; (3) 6 September 2022, hour by hour around the text alert (17:00 to 20:00): demand served, the model's prediction and its band; (4) how sure, on both days: how far the model extrapolates, its error on hot days, and whether the day's figure moves with the model's specification; (5) what the cut of 6 September 2022 was worth at the wholesale price.
**How it was read:** this machine cannot build the site. The whole page was drawn to HTML by its own function, its three reads answered from the tables of this machine (`site/scripts/caiso-alerts-figures.mjs`, session 7532), and read line by line, every chart's labels and every tip; the two heat-wave pages it links were read in their source. Not seen: no click, no chart, no hover in a browser, no layout at 390 px, and not the live set (the worktree has no key; part A's faults file says the same).

Part A's rows (`docs/reviews/caiso-alerts-faults-2026-11-16.md`) are cited by number and not written again. The page's figures are those of `flex_alert_effects` and `flex_alert_model` as retrieved on 2 October 2026 and `caiso_grid_emergencies` as retrieved on 13 October 2026 (the report revised 9 October 2026); every table the page reads is public.

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **The alert message's own effect.** Every figure is the alert evening's whole demand side: the message, the programs, CAISO's own demand response, generators allowed to run, and on 2020-08-14 and 15 rotating outages. The page says so, and since this session says which programs ran in which years (row 4).
- **A figure inside an hour.** The demand series is hourly; the five minutes the EIA measured are inside one hour of the chart (row 10).
- **One day under another model.** The five specifications are pooled only (row 7); the day's interval and "Days as hot" are the page's answer to how sure, and on 2022-09-06 the model has one training day as hot.
- **A value before July 2023.** No hourly CAISO price is held for 2018 to 2022 (rows 8 and 9).
- **CAISO's own estimate against its own baseline,** beyond the two published figures now on the page: CAISO's settlement data for its demand response are not public.
