## The verdict

1. **Computable from public documents for every state:** onsite jobs (the reader's or an announced project's own figures, or defaults from the announced projects of part B, each with its range), onsite earnings (Census County Business Patterns, NAICS 518210, by state, held as `census_cbp_hosting_annual`), the state income tax on them (the tool's own rule), and the sales tax a datacenter exemption forgoes on equipment (Tax Foundation's rates, already the tool's).
2. **Computable where the statute is read:** the datacenter sales tax exemption's thresholds and term (Virginia, Louisiana, Iowa read today; 31 more states named in the note's section 4 to read again with addresses), the property tax rule where a state fixes an assessment ratio or class (Louisiana's 10 and 15 percent, Virginia's separate class with a local rate), and a tax on electricity where a statute sets one (Virginia's consumption tax per kWh; Indiana's and Iowa's exemptions of a qualified datacenter's electricity from sales tax).
3. **Not computable without a paid source:** supply chain and induced effects in any state but Virginia. No free multiplier for this industry exists in public: JEDI has no datacenter model (request 30: twelve model families, none for datacenters); BEA's RIMS II sells them (USD 520 a region, USD 160 an industry for all 50 states and DC, request 35); IMPLAN prints no price (request 39); LSU's LEIM is a subscription web application built on RIMS II (request 26). Virginia is the exception: JLARC's report of December 2024 publishes the Weldon Cooper Center's IMPLAN results for the industry in Virginia, direct, indirect and induced (request 31, tables D-3 and D-5), from which a Virginia ratio of indirect and induced to direct can be computed and cited.
4. **Not computable anywhere without a typed figure:** local property tax levies (every state's rates are local; a statewide default only where a state publishes one), the materials share of a datacenter's building cost (no public document read gives it), and abatements negotiated project by project (payments in lieu, fee agreements).
5. **Order of work:** parts B to D build Virginia, Louisiana and West Virginia first (three announced projects whose own government documents state megawatts, investment and jobs; Virginia's and Louisiana's statutes read; West Virginia's code host does not answer, so its taxes wait), then Indiana, Iowa, Mississippi, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, Georgia, Ohio and Texas in part E, as their statutes can be read.

## 2. The method this is modeled on: LSU's Louisiana Economic Impact Model

Read: https://www.lsu.edu/ces/research/leim.php (request 26), https://www.lsu.edu/ces/research/leap.php (27), the LEAP white paper https://www.lsu.edu/ces/publications/2025/lsu-leap-model-2025-df.pdf (28, Reams, Slack, Snyder and Upton, 2025), the Center's CCS report page (29). www.lsu.edu's robots file disallows only paths not read here.

1. LEIM "is a Leontief style input-output model customized for the Louisiana economy", with "special attention ... to the geographic distribution of economic impacts and tax impacts" (request 26).
2. Its multipliers are BEA's: "LEIM is based on the Regional Input-Output Modeling System (RIMS II) that is constructed by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)."
3. It allocates impacts "across parishes of the state using gravity models of trade, producing parish-specific estimates ... regardless of the initial location".
4. Taxes are explicit: "LEIM also considers Louisiana tax structure explicitly ... Local tax impacts are based on specific information about property and sales tax down to an individual parish."
5. It has a property tax module for a new investment "taking into account Louisiana's Industrial Tax Exemption Program (ITEP)".
6. Inputs, as the CCS report page describes its use: announced projects' investment, from which employment and output are estimated (request 29).
7. Outputs: jobs, output, and state and local tax revenue, statewide and by parish.
8. Access: "LEIM is available through a web application", for subscribers (request 26). It is not public and is not used; nothing of it is copied.
9. LEAP (request 28) places LEIM as its third component, "Economic & Tax Impact Modeling", between structured listening and communication back to the community, and states the aim as neither "to convince the community to move forward (or not) with a project" nor to negotiate: the same neutrality as this tool.
10. What this tool takes from it: the project as inputs (investment, jobs, place, years), effects by year, taxes from each state's own statutes with the abatement as an input; what it cannot take: RIMS II's multipliers (paid, section 3.4) and the parish allocation (it needs them).
