Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), session 62. Seven U.S. ISO regions, September 2025 to August 2026, measured the same way: what a flat 1 GW load would pay for energy, how much carbon it would carry, how stressed the grid is, how long new supply takes to connect, and how much the region leans on its neighbors. Every number below is a row of the table ai_power_regions, read from Supabase with its check key. Five findings Energy cost differs by a factor of 1.11 . At real-time hub prices, a flat 1 GW would have paid 249.06 million USD a year in CAISO, the cheapest region, and 276.14 million USD in ERCOT, the dearest: 27.07 million USD more, for wholesale energy alone. Carbon differs more than cost. The same gigawatt would carry 1,250,885.79 tonnes of CO2 a year in CAISO and 3,600,882.64 in SPP, 2.88 times as much ( 2,349,996.85 tonnes more). CAISO is both the cheapest and the cleanest; it is also the last of 4 to connect new supply. The three cheapest are CAISO, SPP, ERCOT; the three cleanest CAISO, ERCOT, PJM. New supply connects fastest in ERCOT. Requests that came online in 2018 to 2025 took a median 3.79 years from request to operation there, against 7.79 in CAISO, 4.00 years longer. ERCOT completed the largest share of its 2000 to 2019 requests, 30.27 percent; CAISO the smallest, 12.98 percent. The queues are not the bottleneck in size. ERCOT alone held 408,002.06 MW of active requests at the end of 2025, and CAISO 190,717.13 MW. What matters is how little of it gets built, and how long it takes. Some regions lean on their neighbors. Over the year, CAISO imported a net 27.02 percent of its demand (EIA's own balance, which reads high for California from 16 December 2025, as the note at the top says; the pair sums give 16.64 percent) and was a net importer on 100 percent of its complete days. PJM is at the other end: -3.00 percent (a negative share is a net exporter). A new gigawatt in an importing region adds to that reliance unless new supply comes with it. 1. The question, and the unit A large AI campus is, to the grid, a steady load: hundreds of megawatts to a gigawatt and more, nearly flat around the clock. Where it lands decides what it pays for energy, what it emits, whether its evenings are tight, and how soon new generation can be built to serve it. This draft measures the seven ISO regions that run U.S. wholesale markets the same way, over the same year (September 2025 to August 2026), for the same load: a flat 1 GW, 8,760 GWh a year. It does not rank sites: a site sits inside a utility, behind substations, under local rules. It asks which regions start from a better place on five things the warehouse holds. PJM, the largest region by load, is in the carbon, interconnection and import measures but not in cost or price stress: the warehouse licenses PJM's prices for internal use only and does not hold its hub prices. Nothing is estimated in its place. 2. CAISO had the fewest hours at or above USD 200/MWh, 19 ; SPP the most, 160 . Hours above USD 1,000 were rare everywhere: CAISO had the most, 1 . A steady load in a region with many dear hours either pays them or hedges them; it also adds to the demand in the hours that are already tight. For a flat load the dear hours are not optional. A factory or a campus that runs every hour buys the scarcity hours with the rest, and a region with hundreds of them a year asks either for a hedge (a fixed-price contract, which prices the risk in) or for flexibility: a campus that can shift or shed part of its load in those hours, or carry batteries, pays less and eases the grid at the same time. The count of hours above USD 200/MWh is therefore a rough guide to how much a buyer would gain from being able to step back when the grid is short. Grid emergencies are held for CAISO only: 39 days with an ISO-wide Flex Alert or emergency from July 2018 to April 2025. The event studies add the scale of the worst weather the warehouse has measured: the largest effect on daily demand was 8.78 percent above normal, in ERCOT during the summer 2023 heat, after temperature is accounted for where the study holds it. A region that runs short in its extremes is where a new flat load matters most, and where it should bring its own flexibility or supply. 5. ERCOT turned requests into operating plants fastest, a median 3.79 years; CAISO slowest, 7.79 years. Most requests never get built: of the requests made from 2000 to 2019, ERCOT completed 30.27 percent and CAISO 12.98 percent. ISO-NE has no median here: Berkeley Lab gives no online dates for its completed requests. The ISOs' own queues, retrieved in September 2026, show the same order of size as Berkeley Lab's end-2025 counts, and differ where requests were added, withdrawn or counted differently since. A queue is a list of hopes. The completion rate and the time to connect are the better guides to how soon a region could add the supply a new gigawatt of load needs. These are times for generators; a load's own connection runs through its utility and is not in these data. The time to connect matters twice for a new campus. It sets how soon the generation the campus contracts for can be built, and in most regions it also sets how soon the transmission upgrades that new supply triggers get done. Where requests come online in a median 3.79 years (ERCOT), a buyer can match new load with new supply within one planning cycle; where they take 7.79 (CAISO), the buyer relies on existing plants, and on the grid's margin, for most of that time. 6. Putting it together No single number says where a gigawatt should go: a buyer who cares most about cost, carbon or speed would choose differently. The table counts, for each region, how many of the five measures (cost, carbon, scarcity hours, time to connect, imports) put it in the better half of the regions held. It is not a score: the measures are not weighted, and a region missing a measure (PJM's cost and stress, ISO-NE's time to connect) is counted only on what it has. The tension is in the data: the cleanest region, CAISO, is the last of 4 to connect new supply, and the fastest, ERCOT, is the second of 4 on carbon. A campus that wants all three brings its own: a contract for new clean supply where connection is fast, or flexibility that lets it avoid the tight hours where they are many. 8. What this cannot say Permitting and local approval are out of scope. Land, zoning, water, community consent and the time a utility takes to connect a large load are what most often decide a site, and none of them is in these data. Cost is wholesale energy at a hub. Capacity, transmission, distribution, ancillary services, taxes and hedges are left out; they can double a delivered price and differ more by region than energy does. One year. 2025 to 2026's weather and gas prices shaped its prices and scarcity hours; another year would rank some regions differently. Averages, not margins. The carbon of the next MWh, and the price of the hours a new load would add, are not the year's averages. Queues and connection times are for generators. They say how fast a region adds supply, not how fast a data center gets its wires. Regions are large. A region's hub, its average intensity and its net imports hide differences within it, across zones and utilities. PJM's cost and price stress are not here, because its prices are licensed for internal use only and its hub prices are not held. Methods appendix The table is ai_power_regions , built by warehouse/derived/ai_power_regions.py ; the measures, the hubs, the windows and the sources are in docs/reports/ai_gigawatts_methods.md . In short: Cost : cost_of_power_monthly's real-time and day-ahead simple means, weighted by the hours priced, over the twelve months; a flat 1 GW is that price times 8,760,000 MWh. Carbon : carbon_intensity_daily's consumption-based intensity, the mean over the days held; times 8,760,000 MWh for the tonnes. Stress : hourly means of the hub's real-time prices (iso_hub_prices_history; ERCOT ercot_all_hub_prices_history), hours at or above USD 200 and 1,000/MWh; CAISO's emergency days as the Flex Alert scorecard reads caiso_grid_emergencies; the largest pooled effect of a studied event (event_study_estimates, temperature-controlled where held). Connection : Berkeley Lab and GridTracker's Queued Up 2026 data file (lbnl_interconnection_queue, CC BY 4.0): active requests at the end of 2025, the median years from request to operation for requests online in 2018 to 2025, the share of 2000 to 2019 requests completed; and each ISO's own queue (September 2026). Imports : the headline is EIA's balance, demand less net generation over demand, from the region's EIA-930 workbook. The per-day measures sum EIA-930 daily interchange for every pair (eia930_daily_interchange, Eastern days, 2019 on) on complete days, with impossible pair-days (further than ten median absolute deviations from the pair's median) left out.
