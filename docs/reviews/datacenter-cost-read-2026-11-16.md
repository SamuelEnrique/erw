# What a datacenter pays read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, sessions 7521 and 7524, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** the energy lead of an AI lab sizing a 300 MW training site, before a call with a utility or a developer. The page's own questions are hers: what the power costs at a site, whether it is there when it is needed, how clean it is, how soon it can be had, and two sites or two contracts side by side. The All grids tab was read for the same reader by chain 302 (`docs/reviews/allgrids-read-2026-11-16.md`, 17 rows) and is not read again here; this read is the default view, "Your load" (`/cost-of-power`), and its downloads.
**Five cases:** (1) 300 MW at Abilene, Taylor County, Texas, in ERCOT's West load zone (`LZ_WEST`), running flat, bought in real time and day-ahead, then off in its dearest 15 percent of hours: the last twelve months' price, the year's bill, the bad month, the price per GPU-hour; (2) the same site off in its 50 dearest hours a year, with ERCOT's four summer peaks counted: what the flexibility saves, transmission included; (3) Dallas and Fort Worth (ERCOT's North load zone, `LZ_NORTH`) as scenario A against Abilene as scenario B: two sites side by side, on cost, carbon and time to connect; (4) Santa Clara, California (CAISO's NP15 hub) against Southern California (SP15): the same 300 MW in the other large grid the page prices; (5) Loudoun County, Virginia (PJM's Dominion zone): what the page says to the reader whose first site is there.
**How it was read:** Energy Research Warehouse (ERW), page `/cost-of-power`, in review. This machine cannot build the site. The page was drawn to HTML by its own code (`site/app/cost-of-power/page.tsx` through React's static renderer), its reads of the live set answered in the process from the warehouse's table files on this machine, read only (`site/scripts/cost-of-power-read.mjs`, eleven addresses); every cell and hover read as text. No click was made and no chart was seen. The price files are the page's own (`site/data/datacenter/`).
