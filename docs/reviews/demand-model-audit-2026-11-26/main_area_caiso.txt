== titles
A year's demand is what was metered that year, hot summer and cold snap included. A grid whose 2025 was mild shows less growth than its customers added, and a single heat wave can set a peak. These are differences between years, not a trend line.
Demand at the grid is not sales to customers. EIA's retail sales moved with the Lower 48's demand, and apart from it in California and New England. From 2019 to 2025: United States retail sales +7.05 percent, the Lower 48's demand here +7.03; Texas +24.20, ERCOT +27.22; New York -1.26, NYISO -2.73; New England +0.14, ISO-NE -2.54; California -2.96, CAISO +4.45. A state is not a balancing authority, and sales leave out line losses. California: EIA's demand counts the power the batteries take to charge, which is not a sale to a customer. Where that charging is held (CAISO's own data, from June 2025): June to December 2025, demand here +2.81 percent on the same months of 2019, the charging 1,690 MW on average, demand less the charging -3.60 percent, retail sales -2.28 percent; January to August 2026, demand +10.75 percent, the charging 2,114 MW, demand less the charging +2.05 percent, retail sales -2.90 percent. The charging is more than all of the growth of June to December 2025 and 81 percent of that of January to August 2026; it does not close 2026's gap to sales.
