# `/events` and its nine event pages read as the reader they are for (chain 304, part B, session 7549, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** an operator's or a regulator's analyst (a reliability analyst at an ISO, or a FERC or state commission staff member writing an event review) reading a named event start to finish and checking each figure against the operator's own account.
**The five cases:** (1) Winter Storm Uri, ERCOT, 7 to 24 February 2021: ERCOT's highest hour of demand and the load it shed, against FERC's report; (2) the September 2022 California heat, 31 August to 9 September 2022: CAISO's record hour, the day of its Energy Emergency Alert 3, and the heat itself, against CAISO's own account; (3) Winter Storm Elliott, 19 to 29 December 2022: PJM's and SPP's highest hours against PJM's report and the FERC and NERC inquiry; (4) the same three events' figures as `/demand`, `/grid/caiso` and `/learn/problems` give them (CAISO's 51,104 MW, Uri's 69,215 MW and USD 9,051.55, Elliott's 135,328 MW and 41.76 percent); (5) Elliott: how much of PJM's extra demand on 23 and 24 December was the cold, from the event study block.
**How it was read:** this machine cannot build the site. The ten pages were drawn whole with the site's own code (`site/scripts/events-pages.mjs`, run with `./scripts/events-page-register.mjs`) twice: as a visitor's build draws them, the hold of 21 October as it stands, and with the hold set aside for the drawing only (`EVENTS_DRAW_UNHELD=1`), so every figure could be read and checked. Both drawings were made on the public rows beside the replication (`replication/events/window_rows.csv`, the build of 19 October 2026 with the 484 rows of ISO-NE's, MISO's and NYISO's hubs left out), so no held figure was drawn. Each case was read line by line, every sentence, every tip of the decisions block and the charts' point data. Not seen: no click, no hover in a browser, no layout at 390 px, and not the live set's rows (no key in the worktree; part A's faults file, row 6).

Part A's rows (`docs/reviews/events-faults-2026-11-16.md`) and its cross-check (`docs/reviews/events-crosscheck-2026-11-16.md`) are cited by number and not written again.
