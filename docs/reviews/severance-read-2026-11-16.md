# The severance tool read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7511, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a small operator of low-producing wells, or whoever files and checks its monthly production tax reports. Chosen over the mineral owner because every input of the page is an operator's (a well's monthly volume, its completion date, cost ratio, water cut, New Mexico production unit district), the Lease tab speaks of "your wells", and no input is an owner's share; the owner's question is taken as case 5, since the operator's statement is what the owner reads.
**The five cases:** (1) a Panhandle gas well in Gray County, Texas, at 60 Mcf a day: what does it owe in August and September 2026 with the low-producing gas well credit? (2) a three-well oil lease in Wichita County, Texas, at 8 barrels a well a day: does it earn the low-producing oil lease credit in August to October 2026? (3) a well near Jal, Lea County, New Mexico, 3,000 bbl and 9,000 Mcf a month with a one-eighth federal royalty: what are the four taxes in August and October 2026, with the district's ad valorem? (4) Louisiana, October 2026: a stripper well in Caddo Parish at 5 barrels a day, and a horizontal Haynesville gas well in De Soto Parish completed on 15 March 2026 making 300,000 Mcf; (5) a well in Reeves County, Texas, at 600 barrels and 1,500 Mcf a day in October 2026: what tax falls on the well's month and on a one-eighth royalty's share of it?
**How real:** the places, the rules, the months and the prices are real (the prices are the month's means of `eia_fuel_spot_prices`); the sizes are stated sizes of the kind each place has, and belong to no named well, lease, operator or owner.

Energy Research Warehouse (ERW). The tool: `/severance` (the calculator), `/severance?tab=lease` (the Lease tab). Part A of this chain took it to the done line (session 7502): its faults are `docs/reviews/severance-faults-2026-11-16.md`, cited here by row and not written again.

**How it was read.** This machine cannot build the site. The calculator (`site/app/severance/Calculator.tsx`) and the lease tool (`site/app/severance/lease/LeaseTool.tsx`) were drawn to HTML with the site's own code (`site/scripts/tsx-loader.mjs`), with the default prices computed by the page's own functions (`lib/leaseprices.ts`) from the rows of `eia_fuel_spot_prices` as held on the data folder of this machine (rows to 11 November 2026). The calculator keeps its inputs in the browser and reads none from the address, so each case was set by giving its inputs their values as the component is drawn: what a reader's choices would leave on the screen. The Lease tab was given each case as a file through its own `initial` input. Every cell, every list entry and every hover of what was drawn was read (18 drawings, kept in `runs/7511/`, not in git). The page's lead and "Every rule, with its source" under the calculator are a server view that does not draw this way: they were read in the code (`site/app/severance/page.tsx`). **Not seen:** no click, no layout, no width of 390 px, no network panel. The internal pages (`/severance/lease/real`, `/severance/finder`) were not drawn for a case: the first is the Lease tab on a lease of an internal table, whose rows may not be named here, and the second is not replicated (part A, row 4). What only a browser shows is row 17.

## Two pages that share a figure

- **`/learn/problems`** computes a month's tax with `/severance`'s own arithmetic and rules on the same table: row 13, a difference of 17 to 86 cents on the same case, from where the month's mean is rounded.
- **`/impact`** reads the same rules file (`gasSeverance()` in `site/lib/forecastdata.ts`) and the same arithmetic (`compute`), so its rates are `/severance`'s by construction (`docs/methods/impact_tax_rules.md` holds the 40 base, fee and option rows of `impact_tax_rules` equal to the file, 40 of 40). It differs in two choices around them: rows 14 and 15. Both are for the owner's ruling.
- **`/board` and `/prices`** read the same rows of `eia_fuel_spot_prices` through the same reader and draw the last day's value and its moves; neither draws a month's mean, so there is no figure in common to differ. The Digest's fuel lines are a day's close from the same table.
- **The two tabs of the tool** were set against each other on cases 1, 3 and 4: with the same month's price they agree to the cent (USD 389.21; USD 23,112.77 and USD 18,998.35; USD 1,977.61, USD 494.40 and USD 45,420.00). Rows 3 and 5 were the two places they did not say the same thing.

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **The price at the well.** Every default is a hub price. The state's first purchase price of crude is public and not yet in the warehouse (row 12); Waha's gas price needs a license (row 11); a well's own price is on its statement, and the page takes it when typed.
- **A credit or a rate for a month after the rules were read.** The Texas certified prices stop at August 2026 and New Mexico's table at 31 August 2026 (part A, rows 2 and 5): September's gas credit, 100 percent, is not computed. The rules file is re-read by the data machine, not on a clock.
- **Whether a well qualifies.** A flag is the file's numbers against a threshold. The state's certification, payout of the well cost, wellhead pressure and a credit already claimed are in no table the ERW holds.
- **The whole of what is owed.** New Mexico's reduced rates, Louisiana's and New Mexico's fees, Texas's marketing cost deduction for gas and New Mexico's equipment tax are not held (row 10 and the method note's "Left out"), so a Louisiana or New Mexico month is the tax without its fees.
- **An owner's net.** The page computes the tax on a volume. Who bears it, an owner's decimal and the costs taken off a royalty before the tax are in no rule the page has read (rows 9 and 10): a royalty owner can check the tax line of a statement by typing his share's volume and price, and nothing else on it.
