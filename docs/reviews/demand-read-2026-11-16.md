# Demand growth (`/demand`, its three tabs) read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part C, session 7543, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a utility planner (load forecasting, a PJM member utility) reading a grid's load, its peaks, its weather response and its growth over named months, for PJM and the grids beside it. Not the explorer `/explore/demand-growth`, which is another tool, read by session 7517.
**The five cases:** (1) PJM, 2019 to 2025 and January to October 2026: average and peak growth, and the record hour of 2 July 2026, against `/grid`'s Records; (2) ERCOT's summer peak of 22 July 2026 (91,075 MW): how much of the growth since 2019 to 2021 the weather explains; (3) SPP's year's energy of 2025, as metered (+11.14 percent on 2019) and with the weather taken out (+12.5, give or take 1.5, on 2019 to 2021); (4) CAISO, January to October 2026 (+10.98 percent on the same months of 2019) beside California's retail sales on the consumption tab (+0.1 percent, September 2025 to August 2026); (5) Virginia (Dominion's zone) and New Mexico on the consumption tab, the twelve months to August 2026, and a zone's own load growth.
**How it was read:** this machine cannot build the site. Every view was drawn to HTML by the page's own function (`site/app/demand/page.tsx`, with the stand-in for `next/font` of `site/scripts/battery-awards-page-register.mjs`) from the site's committed copies (`site/data/demand_growth.json` built 6 November 2026, `site/data/demand_weather.json` built 6 October 2026); the consumption tab's two live-set reads were answered from this machine's copies of the two EIA tables, cut to the live set's window of 850 days. Every hover and tip was read in the drawn HTML. Not seen: no click, no chart, no hover in a browser, no layout at 390 px, and not the rows of the live set itself.

The tool's done-line files are the other machine's (chain 301): `docs/methods/demand_growth.md` (its decisions, cross-checks and sign-off), `docs/methods/demand_weather.md`, `docs/methods/consumption.md`, `replication/demand/` and the demand section of `docs/reviews/desktop-tools-signature-pack-2026-11-16.md`. They have no faults file of their own; none of their rows is written again here.

## Two pages, one figure

- **`/grid`, Mix, Records, "Peak hour of demand":** the same MW and the same hour as this page's peak (and, for 2026, its highest hour so far) in all 56 grid-years both hold, seven grids from 2019 to 2026 (`site/data/mixplus/*.json` against `site/data/demand_growth.json`; held by the test).
- **`/explore/demand-growth`:** reads `eia930_demand_growth` from the live set; session 7517 found all 24,116 values of this page's copy equal to the table's rows. The two now carry the same line on retail sales and say an hour the same way (rows 5 and 6).
- **The All grids tab (`/cost-of-power?view=allgrids`):** computes its growth from this page's file over another period; this page now says so with the tab's figure (row 12). Its peak of the last whole year (`grid_peak_vs_capacity`) is this table's 2025 peak for all seven grids (session 7517).
- **The weather tab against the as-metered tab:** every summer peak whose year's peak fell in June to September is the same hour and MW (34 of 34); the year's energy differs by up to 172 MW, now said on each cell (row 2).

## What a planner still cannot get from the page, and why

- **A zone's own load:** Dominion's, AEP's, ComEd's (row 15). The table exists; it waits on an upload only the owner makes.
- **The balancing authorities outside the seven** (TVA, Southern, Duke, Bonneville, Florida Power and Light): not held since 2019 (the method's decision 9; a pull of the same public record).
- **A forecast.** The page holds none; the explorer's foot links where the ERW holds forecast peaks (the All grids tab's Headroom).
- **A weather-normal summer peak for 2026** in five of seven grids (row 17), and the weather view's months after 27 September 2026 (row 14).
- **The instant peak.** An hour's average reads below an operator's own record (decision 5).
