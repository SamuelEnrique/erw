## The terms check as session 8403 wrote it

What this is: the five evidence groups read each host's robots file before a page, but did not read every host's terms of use. This check closes that gap after the fact. It is a plain reading by a session, not legal advice, and it changes no evidence file.

How it was done:

- The hosts are those of `runs/c503/hosts_taxes.md`, `hosts_storage.md`, `hosts_prices.md`, `hosts_grid.md` and `hosts_trackers.md` from which a page or a document was read. Refused hosts and hosts whose robots file alone was read are left out (list at the end).
- A reading already on record counts and is cited: `docs/licenses.md` (the numbered reads, dated in `warehouse/metadata/license_reads.csv`), the method notes, or a group's hosts file of today.
- Where no reading was on record, the terms, conditions, disclaimer or policy page the host's own footer links was read today, its robots file first. Scripts, run by name: `runs/c503/terms_robots.py` (robots files, kept in `runs/c503/terms_robots/`), `runs/c503/terms_fetch.py` (pages, raw and text, kept in `runs/c503/terms_pages/`), `runs/c503/terms_links.py` (footer links of saved pages, no request), `runs/c503/terms_grep.py` (the lines on automated access and on reuse, no request), `runs/c503/terms_text.py` (text of a page another agent saved, no request). Outputs: `terms_robots1.out`, `terms_robots2.out`, `terms_fetch1.out` to `terms_fetch3.out`, `terms_links.out`, `terms_links2.out`, `terms_grep1.out`, `terms_grep2.out`.
- Requests made by this check: 15 for robots files and 38 for pages, one request an address, no retry, client name `research-reader/1.0`, no personal name or address. No model API was called. No request went to misoenergy.org, scc.virginia.gov, icc.illinois.gov, dis.puc.state.oh.us, sec.gov, comptroller.texas.gov, ERCOT's `/misapp` or `/misdownload`, or a host a hosts file lists as refused.
- Said plainly, two things this check did beyond a terms page alone. (1) For 13 hosts no saved page showed a footer, so the home page was asked once, only to find the footer's link; nothing else is taken from it. (2) Where a host links a privacy policy and no terms page, the privacy policy on the same host was read, to see whether it speaks of automated reading or reuse.
- Six pages already on record were read again today (CPUC's conditions of use, the Department of Energy's web policies, the Louisiana Legislature's disclaimer, New Mexico Taxation and Revenue's privacy page, the IRS's privacy policy, NCEI's privacy page), because the record gives their words on reuse and not on automated access.

42 hosts. Verdicts: 23 "allows or is silent", 1 "forbids automated access", 1 "asks permission for reuse", 3 "terms not readable", 14 "no terms page found".

### Hosts whose terms forbid automated access or ask permission

Two hosts. Entry numbers are the evidence files `runs/c503/evidence/NNN.md` that cite a page read from the host.

- **`www.pge.com` (Pacific Gas and Electric): forbids automated access, and asks written consent for use in a publication or on another website.** One entry: **61** (the wholesale month of the bill page). What it took from the host: Electric Rule No. 9, Sheet 1, section A, on how a bill's period runs (one meter reading to the next, 27 to 33 days counted as a month). The entry's recommendation is "no basis to recommend", and it does not rest on that page: it rests on what was looked for and not found (no publisher weights a wholesale price over a bill's read days, no ruling, no method rule). The block also cites EIA's Table 5.1 and `docs/methods/bill.md`. Taking the PG&E sentence out would leave the recommendation as it is. Two things to weigh: the document is a tariff sheet filed with the California Public Utilities Commission, and the robots file allows its path; the terms speak of information "displayed on the Website" and make no exception for the tariff book. Entry 116 names PG&E's credit, but from the commission's page, not from this host.
- **`www.revenue.wi.gov` (Wisconsin Department of Revenue): asks permission for a copy or use beyond fair use; says nothing against reading by a program.** One entry: **34** (Wisconsin's solar sales tax exemption). What it took from the host: Publication 207's section on wind, gas and solar powered products, in the entry's own words, no quotation from it. The recommendation ("keep") does not rest on that page alone: the block's first document is the Department's rule itself, Tax 11.10(3) and (4), read from `docs.legis.wisconsin.gov`, and the recommendation cites Tax 11.10(4)(c) first; Publication 207 repeats the same two lists. Without Publication 207 the recommendation stands on the rule.

No other host's terms, as read or as recorded, forbid automated access or ask permission for the use made today (a fact or a short phrase, cited).
