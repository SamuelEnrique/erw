## What published figures stand against

The cross-check is section 6 of `docs/methods/ercot_peak_premium.md`, eight rows. In short: the plain mean of the 15-minute prices at the hub average is 12 to 26 percent below the market monitor's load-weighted average real-time price in each year of 2020 to 2024, the direction load-weighting gives, and the five years rank the same; its account of the tightest hours moving from 4 to 5 p.m. to 7 to 9 p.m. agrees with the peak median pulling away from the midday median (from -0.16 in 2015 to 14.52 in 2025); its "hundreds of hours" at or below zero agrees with 240 hours here in 2025; the highest prices of 2022 to 2024 sit at its 5,000 USD offer cap. Nothing published stands against the peak-block IQR, the spread of the two medians, the block quartiles, the 99.9th percentile or the worst-interval multiple: they are this page's own measures. The 2025 average of the 2025 report could not be read on this machine, and the EIA's article on 2025 prices names no ERCOT price.
