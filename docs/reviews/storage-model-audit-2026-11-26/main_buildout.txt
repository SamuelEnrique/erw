Added in twelve months is net of retirements. Planned is every battery unit developers have reported to EIA, by the year they expect it online; planned energy (MWh) is not held, because EIA's monthly inventory gives none for planned units. PJM is included: these are EIA's public generator data, not PJM's prices. How generators are assigned to a grid, and how many were not A generator belongs to the grid named by its balancing authority code in EIA's inventory: CISO is CAISO, ERCO is ERCOT, ISNE is ISO-NE, NYIS is NYISO, SWPP is SPP, and MISO and PJM are themselves. Any other code, or none, is not assigned to one of the seven. Not assigned: 260 operating battery units, 16,038 MW, of the 1,156 units and 56,121 MW in the United States. Most sit in the West and Southeast outside any ISO. They are in the United States total and in the table's row "Outside the ISOs"; the total is the seven grids plus that row. The method counts how many of them carry no code at all, and gives the same counts for solar and planned units. What EIA-860M covers and misses Form EIA-860M is the monthly inventory of utility-scale generators: units at plants of 1 MW and above. Batteries behind a customer's meter and rooftop solar are not in it. Planned dates are the developers' own estimates. They slip, and some planned units are never built: read the planned columns as intentions, not a forecast. The years are rebuilt from the one inventory held, by each unit's first operating month. A battery retired before 2025 is in no year, and a unit shows its present MW and MWh in every year it operated, so early years are slightly understated. The newest month held September 2026. EIA publishes each month's inventory near the end of the following month, and units that started late are added to later inventories, so the newest months grow a little when the next one arrives. Decisions on this page (each with its default and its source on hover) Battery output is net generation as published: positive while the batteries discharge to the grid, negative while they charge, so a day's charging is the part below zero taken as a positive amount. (session 31) i Default: Positive discharging, negative charging. Source: EIA-930's battery series (fuel type BAT) and CAISO's Today's Outlook, both signed this way. EIA's hourly period is the hour's end in UTC, so each hour is dated by its start, one hour before EIA's stamp; CAISO's hour is the mean of its twelve 5-minute values. (session 31) i Default: Every hour dated by its start. Source: EIA API v2, electricity/rto/fuel-type-data; CAISO's 5-minute battery history. A day is the balancing authority's local day and counts only when every hour of it is held (23 or 25 on the clock-change days; for CAISO every 5-minute interval); a day short of one is left out, never filled. (session 31) i Default: Whole local days only. Source: docs/methods/storage.md, "Days" and "Completeness". Each authority's day is in its own time: Central for ERCOT and SPP, Pacific for CAISO, Eastern for ISO-NE and for the Lower 48 (as EIA's Grid Monitor shows it), and fixed EST for MISO, as MISO publishes. (session 31) i Default: Each grid's own local time; MISO on fixed EST. Source: The operators' market time zones; EIA's Grid Monitor default. The day's energy out over energy in is not a measured efficiency: charge held across midnight moves it, and so do batteries reported under other fuels (solar or wind with integrated storage). (session 31) i Default: Shown as out over in, labelled not an efficiency. Source: docs/methods/storage.md, the daily cycle's notes. California is drawn from CAISO's own 5-minute battery data, not EIA-930's, which CAISO does not report as BAT; it includes the batteries of hybrid plants, which CAISO publishes inside its total. (session 34) i Default: CAISO's own data for California, labelled as CAISO's. Source: CAISO, Today's Outlook, battery storage history ("Total batteries"). The fleet is every generator with EIA's prime mover "Batteries" in EIA-860M, at its nameplate MW and, where EIA gives it, its nameplate energy; the page's sums by status, grid, state and planned year are sums of those rows and nothing else. (session 31) i Default: EIA-860M battery units at nameplate; sums of the rows only. Source: EIA-860M, Monthly Update to the Annual Electric Generator Report. Energy capacity in MWh is EIA's own figure, carried for operating and retired units; planned units have none in EIA-860M, and a unit's MWh is never estimated from its MW. (session 34) i Default: MWh only where EIA states it. Source: EIA-860M, "Nameplate Energy Capacity (MWh)". A unit's grid is the ISO of its balancing authority when that is one of the seven; any other unit stands outside the ISOs with its own authority's code, never assigned by its state. (session 31) i Default: The ISO of the unit's balancing authority, else outside the ISOs. Source: EIA-860M's balancing authority code for each generator.
