# The ERCOT peak premium explorer read as the reader it is for (chain 304, part B, session 7516, 16 November 2026)

**The reader:** a fund analyst sizing the evening premium a merchant battery or a peaker captures in ERCOT. Why this one and not the developer choosing between two nodes: the page holds six hubs and no node, sets two years side by side and reads one calendar month across the years, so its question is how far the evening block's level and spread have moved, an underwriter's question; the developer's question is case 1, taken as far as hubs go.
**The five cases:** (1) HB_WEST against HB_NORTH: the peak block's median and spread in 2025, and in August 2025; (2) how the evening premium moved from 2019 to 2025 at HB_HUBAVG; (3) February 2021 (Winter Storm Uri) at HB_HOUSTON: what the month shows and what it does to 2021's figures; (4) the worst interval of 2023 at HB_HUBAVG and its multiple of the median; (5) 2026 so far against 2025 at HB_HUBAVG: is it a comparison of like months.
**How it was read:** this machine cannot build the site. The page (`/explore/ercot-peak-premium`) was drawn to HTML with the site's own code for 14 views (the opening and the five cases' views; `site/scripts/test-peak-premium-read.mjs` draws the fixed ones again), its reads answered from the two tables as this machine holds them (`ercot_peak_premium_annual`, `ercot_peak_premium_monthly`, built 29 September 2026: 2026 runs to 28 September), and every cell and every hover of what was drawn was read. No click was made and no chart was seen: the three charts' points, their hover cards and their "Table" views are not in the server's HTML. Part A's rows (`docs/reviews/ercot-peak-premium-faults-2026-11-16.md`, session 7507) are cited by number and not written again.

## Other pages that share a figure with this one

- **The ERCOT tab of `/prices`** (`site/app/explorer/ercot-peak-premium/`) draws the same rows of the same two tables, one hub and one year at a time. Compared on case 1 by its code: every figure is the same row, written by the same rule (two decimals, a count whole). **For the desktop's tool:** the whole-dollar price that part A suspected (its row 14) is not a fault there: `fmt` in `Explorer.tsx`, lines 35 to 38, writes every price with two decimals (25.00). The tab marks a partial year in its title and with its count; it has no month view and no second hub.
- **`/grid`** does not draw the annual table. Its ERCOT panel draws the daily on-peak and off-peak means of `price_board_peak_offpeak`: another block, another statistic, another market and another period (row 14). No figure to compare.
- **`/board`** links to the ERCOT tab ("ERCOT since 2015: the peak premium explorer") and draws no row of the two tables. The yearly on-peak means of `price_board_peak_offpeak` (`rt_year_peak_mean`) are drawn on no page today (only the retired board drew them), so the page's medians stand beside no mean on the site.
- **`/cost-of-power/battery`** reads `battery_stack_monthly` and `node_basis_summary`: revenue by month and the storage nodes' four-hour day-ahead spread. No median by block is drawn there; no figure to compare. It is where row 5 sends the developer.

## What this reader still cannot get from the tool, and why

- **A node.** The tables are computed for the six hubs. A named node's evening premium is on no page; the battery page gives the band of storage nodes against the hub, day-ahead, for twelve months (rows 5 and 6).
- **Dollars a battery would have earned.** The page gives the level and the spread of prices by block, not the revenue of a charge and a discharge: a median spread is not a daily spread captured. That figure is the battery page's, from another table and by another rule.
- **A figure a counterparty would recognise.** Every central figure is a median of a 16:00 to 21:00 block of every day; the market monitor publishes load-weighted means and the traded block is 06:00 to 22:00 on weekdays (part A's row 11, its ruling 4).
- **The current year on equal terms, and current.** 2026 so far has no like figure for 2025 beside it (row 9), and the tables are computed only on the data machine: 26 days old on the day of the read (part A's row 6).
- **When, and hour by hour.** Three blocks, the same hours every day: no hour-by-hour profile, no weekday against weekend, no day or hour of an extreme (row 8), and the years between two chosen years only as points of a chart (row 7).
