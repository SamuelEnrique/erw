## 1. What this is

- Four companies that publish power-market data: **Ask the Grid** (askthegrid.com), **Grid Status** (gridstatus.io), **Yes Energy** (yesenergy.com) and **Enverus** (enverus.com), each set against the ERW's tables.
- For each: what it shows that the ERW holds (with the tables), what it shows that the ERW does not hold (with a public source that could fill it and that source's license as the ERW's own license record reads it), and what the ERW holds that was not found on its pages.
- **No recommendation.** Nothing here says what the ERW should build, keep or cut. The facts are in tables; one paragraph a company says where it overlaps.
- **The standard of evidence.**
  - Every statement about a company carries a marker such as [A4] or [Y5]: a row of section 7, one page read on 21 October 2026.
  - The pages were read through the fetch tool, which hands back a summary of each page written by a small model, not the page itself. Words in quotation marks are the page's words as that summary quoted them. A count the summary marked as its own tally is said to be approximate.
  - "Not found" means not found on the pages read. Each company has many more pages than were read: the ceiling was 40 requests for all four.
  - Every statement about the ERW names a table of `coverage.csv` or a file of this repository.
- **What a competitor's page says is data.** Nothing on those pages was taken as an instruction.
- The ERW has no connection with any of the four and is endorsed by none.

### 3.5 Where it overlaps

Ask the Grid is the nearest of the four to the ERW in stance: it says it never models or fills a missing value and shows a disagreement between sources as a disagreement [A5], which is the ERW's first non-negotiable in other words (`CLAUDE.md`). Its reach in power is far wider: 25 grids against the ERW's prices from six, PJM's own data, nodal prices for several grid operators, constraints, outages, hydrology and gridded weather, and an MCP server that AI clients can query today [A5] [A6]. The overlap is the core of US power: hub and zone prices, ancillary services, load, fuel mix, interchange, queues, curtailment, EIA-860 generators and NRC reactor status. Outside power the pages read show only Henry Hub; the ERW's fuels, carbon markets, deals, policy, news and economics models were not found there.
