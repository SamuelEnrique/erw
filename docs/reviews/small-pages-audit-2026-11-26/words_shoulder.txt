The definitions, exactly as computed The average day: for each local hour, the mean over the month's complete days (every hour of demand, solar and wind held) of EIA-930's hourly demand, solar (SUN and SNB), wind (WND and WNB) and battery output. A month with fewer than 90 percent of its days complete is not shown. Net load: demand less solar and wind. The midday surplus: the run of hours around net load's lowest hour in which net load is below its daily mean; its MWh are the sum, over those hours, of the mean less net load. The evening shoulder: from the first hour after solar's highest hour in which solar is below half of that highest value, to the first hour, after net load has risen above its daily mean, in which net load is back at or below that mean. If net load stays above the mean to midnight, the shoulder ends at midnight and the page says so. Its MWh are the sum, over its hours, of net load less the mean, counting only the hours in which net load is above the mean; its length counts every hour from its start. The fleet: the batteries operating that month in EIA's monthly generator inventory (EIA-860M), their MW and MWh. Its hours are its MWh over its MW. Hours covered is the smaller of the shoulder's length and the fleet's hours; hours needed is the shoulder's MWh above the mean over the fleet's MW. The second measure: the run of hours around the evening's highest net load (the highest from the shoulder's start to midnight) in which net load is above the midpoint between its daily mean and that peak; its MWh are the sum, over those hours, of net load less the midpoint. Hours covered and hours needed are computed against it as against the first. The worst days: each complete day of 24 local hours is measured as the average day is, on its own hours and against its own mean. The ten with the most shoulder energy above the mean in each local year are ranked; a day with no shoulder is not ranked and is not in the count of days ranked. Hours needed on a day is its shoulder energy over the fleet's MW of that month; where the month's fleet is not published yet it is not given, and the year's figure is the mean over all ten or is not given. Batteries discharged is the fleet's output in the shoulder's hours, where every hour of the day holds it; batteries ran is that over the fleet's MW. Demand: an hour of demand that could not have happened (zero, more than a quarter away from the four hours around it, or outside one third to three times the grid's median hour) is a blank, and its day is not a complete day. The arithmetic asks only what the batteries alone could cover. The mean is a choice. A shoulder measured against another level (the evening peak's half, say) would be shorter; the definition is this page's. Decisions on this page (each with its default and its source on hover) The evening shoulder is measured against net load's daily mean: it lasts while net load is above the mean of the day's 24 hours. i Default: The daily mean. A higher level gives a shorter shoulder: the second measure on this page uses the midpoint between the mean and the evening peak. Source: Session 75; docs/methods/shoulder_hours.md, "On the average day". The term is this page's, not an industry standard. The shoulder starts at the first hour after solar's highest hour in which solar is below half of that highest value. i Default: Half of the day's highest solar hour. Source: Session 75; solar is EIA-930's SUN plus SNB (solar with storage), or CAISO's own solar. It ends at the first hour, after net load has risen above the mean, in which net load is back at or below it; when that never comes it is cut at midnight, and the page says so. i Default: Cut at midnight, not carried into the next day.
