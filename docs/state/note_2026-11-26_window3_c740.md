# Note for Samuel: datacenters in the Economic impact tool (`/impact`), chain 740, lab-desktop window 3, 26 November 2026

Written in session 10811 and kept current while part F runs (the chain goes on until the weekly limit).

## What `/impact` can do for a datacenter (once row 14 lands)

- Address: `/impact` (in review). In the left box, **Technology: Datacenter**; pick the **State**.
- **Announced project** (optional): a list of the state's announced datacenters read from their own documents (44 projects); picking one sets its MW, investment, permanent jobs and construction workers as the document states them, with a link to the document.
- **Project**: size (MW, as you state them), investment (USD million, 2025), first construction year, construction and operating years. **Spending**: building, equipment and land shares (JLARC's 19.6, 68.2, 6.1 percent), equipment bought again every 5 years. **Jobs and wages**: permanent jobs, construction workers, earnings per job (Census CBP by state). **Taxes**: sales tax rate, the building's taxable share (not given), the state's exemption applied or not, the property tax rates on the building and on the equipment (the locality's, typed), the equipment's schedule (at cost unless you type a county's, e.g. Loudoun's 60, 15, 10), an abatement and its years, the electricity bought (MWh). Every default is grey with its source and range on hover.
- **Scenario B**: the same fields; e.g. the same project without the exemption, with an abatement, or in another state, or a gas plant beside it.
- **Right side**: the A and B table (jobs, earnings, property tax, sales tax paid, before the exemption and forgone by it, income tax, electricity tax, present values), the year chart and table, the same datacenter's sales, income and electricity taxes in every state, and the CSV, Python and Stata files.

## The states covered and what is blank

- **Exemption applied by its statute's thresholds and term** (23): Virginia, Louisiana, Iowa, Minnesota, South Carolina, Missouri, North Dakota, Maryland, Florida, Idaho, Arizona, North Carolina, Wisconsin, Kansas, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, Georgia (from the act as signed, through 2031; the auditor says 2033), Utah (150,000 square feet or more, not tested: the reader turns it off for a smaller one), Arkansas (USD 100 million, the state's rate), Oklahoma (no threshold, the state's rate; from the state's evaluation, as its statute host refuses), Connecticut (USD 200 million outside the zones, 20 or 30 years; from the commerce department's page), Michigan (USD 250 million and 30 jobs, through 2050, certificates before 2030), Wyoming (USD 5 million, the state's rate). Where a threshold depends on county population (Arizona, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, Georgia), the largest county's is used.
- **Named in the hover, never applied** (9): Nevada (an approval), Illinois (its program takes no applications from 1 July 2026), Massachusetts (no applications since 25 June 2026), Ohio (no new requests since 27 May 2026), Nebraska (ImagiNE closed to data centers, 21 July 2026), Alabama (local abatements, granted case by case), Mississippi (its statute not read from an official text), Indiana (its code built by script), Washington (its statute site refuses).
- **Property**: the state's assessment where read (Louisiana 10 and 15 percent; Montana's class seventeen, 0.9 percent of market value; Georgia 40 percent; New Mexico one third; Ohio 35 percent on the building and nothing on the equipment; Iowa, Minnesota, North Dakota and Wisconsin nothing on the equipment; Kansas 25 percent on the building and nothing on the equipment; South Carolina 6 and 10.5 percent; Wyoming 9.5 percent; Arizona 15 percent; Missouri 32 and 33.3 percent; Nevada 35 percent; Alabama 20 percent; Mississippi 15 percent; Arkansas 20 percent; Illinois one third on the building; Tennessee 40 and 30 percent); every rate is the locality's, so the line is blank until typed.
- **Electricity tax**: Virginia's consumption tax, Illinois' electricity excise tax and Ohio's kilowatt-hour tax (the lower of its monthly tiers and its self-assessing schedule at 45 million kWh a year or more), each on the MWh the reader types (other states tax electricity by price, which the tool does not hold).
- **Not readable, honoured**: Texas (a script-built statute site; its governor's site disallows ClaudeBot; the Comptroller's disallows `/*/`), Ohio's statutes (`Disallow: /`; its pause read from the Governor's bulletin), Illinois's statute host (certificate failure, 403 before; its program read from the commerce department), New York (403), West Virginia's code (does not answer).
