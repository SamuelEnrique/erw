## The three likeliest causes, in order

1. **The compute's disk throughput, spent by the evening's writes and then too slow for page reads that miss the cache.**
   - Evidence for: the onset and the recovery came within three and nine hours of the largest write the live set has had, 958,000 rows in 22 minutes plus a vacuum of a 2.1 GB table. The daily runs' loads of the same day came on top. In the same hours Postgres could not even start pg_cron's own connections to `localhost`, which a slow disk explains and a slow query does not. With 512 MB of buffers against a 2.1 GB table, the pages' reads depend on the disk.
   - Evidence against: the 3-hour gap between the end of the load and the onset is long for a cause that acts at once. A budget that drains over the day and refills slowly fits it; a lock or a long query does not, and none is in the record.
   - What would prove it: the project's "Disk IO budget" and IOPS graphs for 20 October 17:00 to 21 October 06:00 UTC in Supabase's dashboard. A session cannot read those.
2. **The series table's size against the compute's memory.** `node_basis_daily` (958,000 rows) and `ercot_rtm_price_adders` (602,000 rows, no page reads it) are 43% of `series`, and both indexes grow with them. Every read of every page walks the same two indexes. The table outgrew the cache with the load of 20 October (2,063 MB to 2,318 MB). Before it the same reads completed, and after it the margin under the anon role's 3 seconds is thin even on a quiet database (1.42 s for a deep page). This is the standing cause beneath the first: with the cache too small, any spell of slow disk turns into timeouts.
3. **Many reads at once.** The site's reads (ISR, the 15-minute jobs, `/ask`) and a production build arriving together. Evidence: none found. No query older than 30 seconds now; 16 idle connections of `authenticator`; nothing heavy in `erw_health`; branch builds are skipped. It is listed because the cancelled statements are not in `pg_stat_statements` and the time of each cancellation is in Supabase's logs, not in the database.

## For Samuel's ruling

- **The compute tier.** The series table is four times `shared_buffers`. A larger compute (more memory, a higher disk budget) addresses causes 1 and 2 at once. The dashboard's disk graphs for the night would settle it first.
- **`node_basis_daily` in the live set** (session 358): 958,000 rows, a quarter of `series`, for the node basis on the three economics pages. A window (`live_set.yaml`'s `days`), or the pages reading their own files as `/board` does, would take most of it out. This changes what the live set holds, so it is yours.
- **`ercot_rtm_price_adders` out of the live set**: 602,000 rows that no page reads (session 244, under `review_hold`).
- **The anon role's 3 seconds**, against the 8 seconds of the other roles: keep it, since it protects the database; it is listed only because it is where the failures show.
- **Every session's load is now behind `scripts/db_probe.py`** (this chain's rule), and the loader could ask the probe itself before it writes. The same goes for the loader's write rate (a pause between tables) after a whole-table write like `node_basis_daily`'s. Not changed here.
